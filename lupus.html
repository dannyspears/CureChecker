Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for lupus (systemic lupus erythematosus, or SLE), but it can be treated. In lupus the immune system attacks the body's own tissues, including skin, joints and kidneys. Hydroxychloroquine, steroids and immune-calming drugs are the base of care, and four targeted drugs are now FDA approved for lupus or its kidney form. One of them, Gazyva (obinutuzumab), is approved for lupus kidney disease, and the FDA is expected to decide by December 2026 whether to approve it for lupus more broadly. Our view: the biggest news is from cell therapies that wipe out and "reset" B cells. Small studies report remissions that last after other drugs are stopped. They are still experimental, with follow-up measured in months to a few years.

  1. Gazyva may be next.In the 303-person ALLEGORY Phase 3 trial (NEJM, 2026), 76.7% responded on obinutuzumab vs 53.5% on placebo at one year. An FDA decision is expected by December 2026.
  2. CAR-T: remissions in small groups.In Erlangen, Germany, all 8 lupus patients given CD19 CAR-T cells reached remission and stopped immune drugs (NEJM, 2024). At EULAR in June 2026, 5 of 6 on the lower dose of Autolus's obe-cel reached remission.
  3. Off-the-shelf versions are coming.A donor-cell CAR-T (prula-cel) put 54% of evaluable patients in remission at 12 months (company release, Sept 28, 2026). A ready-made antibody, A-319, did similar B-cell depletion in a 12-person trial.
  4. Not every result is a win.Dapirolizumab pegol beat placebo in Phase 3, but only 50% vs 35% responded. A 2026 exercise trial raised fitness but did not ease lupus fatigue.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Care decisions belong with a lupus-experienced medical team.

Oct 2, 2026

Litifilimab for skin lupus: 52-week Phase 2 data (AMETHYST)

At the EADV congress, Biogen reported that 27.2% of people given litifilimab from the start had clear or almost clear skin at week 52, up from 19.0% at week 24. People who switched from placebo at week 24 improved within four weeks. Biogen said no new safety signals appeared. Phase 3 results are expected in the first half of 2027. Company-reported; cutaneous lupus only.

Sep 28, 2026

Donor-derived CAR-T (prula-cel): Phase 1 update

Adicet's off-the-shelf gamma delta CAR-T cells, which target CD20, were given to 22 evaluable patients. At 12 months, 54% were in remission (DORIS) and 50% of those with lupus nephritis had a complete kidney response. All stopped immune drugs. No graft-versus-host disease. Infections occurred in 54%. A company release, not yet peer reviewed.

A-319, a ready-made CD3×CD19 antibody: Phase 1

In 12 people with active SLE, there were no serious treatment-related side effects. At 12 months, 8 of 10 reached low disease activity and 6 of 10 reached remission. Small and uncontrolled.

Treatments that are working

Hydroxychloroquine is the base of care. Four targeted drugs are FDA approved for lupus or lupus nephritis, and cell therapies are in trials.

Hydroxychloroquine (Plaquenil)

Standard
Most people with lupus · tablet

One of the main lupus medicines. The Lupus Foundation of America notes it also helps protect against sun sensitivity. It needs regular eye checks.

Benlysta (belimumab)

FDA approved
SLE Mar 9, 2011 · lupus nephritis Dec 16, 2020

Blocks a signal (BLyS) that keeps B cells alive. Given as an infusion or a shot under the skin, added to standard therapy.

Saphnelo (anifrolumab)

FDA approved
Approved Jul 30, 2021 · weekly autoinjector Apr 24, 2026

Blocks the type I interferon receptor. For adults with moderate to severe SLE on standard therapy. The new under-the-skin pen lets people treat at home.

Lupkynis (voclosporin)

FDA approved
Approved Jan 22, 2021 · lupus nephritis

A twice-daily capsule that calms immune T cells, used with other immune drugs to treat lupus kidney disease.

Gazyva (obinutuzumab)

Approved for LN
Lupus nephritis Oct 17, 2025 · SLE under FDA review

Removes B cells. In the REGENCY trial, 46.4% had a complete kidney response vs 33.1% on standard therapy alone. A decision for non-kidney lupus is expected by December 2026.

CAR-T cell therapy

In trials
Severe lupus that hasn't responded to other drugs

Immune cells are engineered to destroy B cells. Small studies report remissions without other immune drugs, but it isn't approved, needs chemotherapy first in most versions, and long-term results are unknown.

Natural remedies & daily care

No diet or supplement cures lupus. The Lupus Foundation of America says there is no specific lupus diet. Some "immune-boosting" products may make it worse. We rate each by the strength of evidence.

Strong expert consensus Limited / mixed evidence No evidence, or possible harm

Helpful daily care

Sun & UV protection

UV light from the sun or some indoor lights can trigger flares. The Lupus Foundation of America advises broad-spectrum SPF 30+ sunscreen, reapplied every two hours, plus tightly woven clothing and a wide-brimmed hat.

Checking vitamin D

Very low levels are linked to worse outcomes, and the Foundation suggests having levels checked. It isn't proven that supplements change lupus risk.

Regular exercise

A 2026 randomized trial found interval training improved fitness, though not fatigue.

Promoted without evidence, or risky

Alfalfa sprouts & tablets

They contain L-canavanine, which may increase inflammation by stimulating the immune system. Johns Hopkins and the Lupus Foundation say to avoid them.

Echinacea

Sold as an immune booster, which may cause flares. The Lupus Foundation of America does not support its use in lupus.

Garlic supplements

Johns Hopkins lists garlic among things to avoid because it may stimulate an already overactive immune system.

Be careful: some medicines, including sulfa antibiotics (Bactrim, Septra), can raise sun sensitivity or lower blood counts in lupus. Tell every doctor you have lupus, and check any supplement with your care team before starting it.

What causes lupus

Lupus is an autoimmune disease: the immune system attacks healthy cells. The exact cause isn't known. It seems to take a mix of genes, hormones and triggers in the environment.

Step 1 · The setup

Genes plus a trigger

~9 womento 1 man
  • Many genes: common variations in many immune genes each raise risk a little
  • Triggers: sunlight, viral infections, stress and chemicals are suspected
  • Medicines: about 10% of cases are thought to be triggered by a drug
Step 2 · The tipping point

The immune system turns on the body

  • Dying cells aren't cleared away properly, which can set off the immune system
  • B cells make autoantibodies, such as anti-double-stranded DNA, and interferon signals run high
Step 3 · The damage

Inflammation in many organs

  • Skin & joints: rashes, sun sensitivity, joint pain
  • Kidneys: about one in three develop kidney disease (lupus nephritis)
  • Heart, lungs & brain: can also be affected
  • Whole body: fatigue is common
Step 4 · The result

Flares and lasting damage

  • Lupus often comes and goes in flares, which UV light can set off
  • Early treatment and steady control aim to prevent lasting organ damage
B cells are central (Feb 2024): in the Erlangen study, wiping out B cells with CAR-T cells led to remission in all 8 lupus patients, even after new B cells came back, which suggests the immune system can be "reset."
A reset without cells (Sept 2026): after the A-319 antibody, blood tests showed interferon signals dropping across several immune cell types and the B-cell population rebuilding, much like after CAR-T.
Vitamin D and risk (Nov 2025): in the Hopkins Lupus Cohort, people starting with very low vitamin D (under 20 ng/mL) had more deaths and heart events. Recent levels didn't show the link, so it's unclear whether supplements change risk.
