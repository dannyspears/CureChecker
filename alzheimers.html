Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for Alzheimer's today. Two approved antibodies, lecanemab and donanemab, slow early-stage decline by roughly a quarter to a third. That's real but modest, and they carry a risk of brain swelling and bleeding. Our view: the cure will look like prevention. Blood tests will flag the disease years before symptoms, and faster, safer drugs will clear it early.

  1. Finding it earlier: already here.Four Alzheimer's blood tests are FDA-cleared. A p-tau217 blood level can predict decline up to a decade before symptoms, according to data presented at AAIC 2026.
  2. Better amyloid drugs: the next step.Roche's trontinemab uses a "brain shuttle" to cross into the brain faster. At the high dose, 92% of patients had amyloid below the positivity threshold by 28 weeks. Phase 3 and a 1,600-person prevention trial are under way.
  3. Prevention through lifestyle: proven to help.The U.S. POINTER trial showed a structured program of exercise, diet, mental challenge and health checks protects thinking skills. About 45% of dementia is linked to 14 risk factors people can change.
  4. Beyond amyloid: still searching.Tau, inflammation and the APOE4 gene are the next targets. The 2026 publication of the failed semaglutide trials shows that not every promising idea works.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Treatment decisions belong with a neurologist or memory-clinic specialist.

What causes Alzheimer's

Alzheimer's starts quietly, many years before memory loss, when a sticky protein called amyloid builds up between brain cells. Amyloid then sets off tangles of another protein, tau, inside the cells. As the tau spreads, brain cells die.

Step 1 · The hits

Risk adds up

<1% inherited outrightlate-onset
  • Age: the biggest risk factor
  • Genes: APOE4 raises risk, and two copies raise it most. Rare APP, PSEN1 and PSEN2 mutations cause early-onset disease.
  • Health: 14 changeable risks, including hearing loss, high LDL, high blood pressure, inactivity, smoking and diabetes
Step 2 · The tipping point

Amyloid builds, then tau spreads

  • Amyloid can build up for 15–20 years before symptoms
  • Tau tangles spread through memory areas, and how far they've spread tracks with how symptoms worsen
Step 3 · The damage

Connections fail

  • Synapses lost: brain cells stop communicating
  • Inflammation: microglia, the brain's immune cells, turn harmful
  • APOE4 effect: speeds tau spread at lower amyloid levels
  • Blood vessels: damage to small vessels adds to decline
  • Energy: brain cells use glucose less efficiently
Step 4 · The result

Memory and thinking decline

  • Starts with short-term memory, then language, planning and independence
  • Today's drugs only help in the earliest stages
APOE4 (Jan 2026): a new study found the APOE4 gene speeds up tau tangles and their spread, and more so with two copies. That helps explain why carriers decline faster.
Lithium (May 2026): Finnish researchers found that lithium reduced harmful tau changes in lab models and affected other Alzheimer's pathways too. Human trials are needed.
New target (Feb 2026): Indiana University scientists showed that removing the enzyme IDOL reduced both amyloid plaques and APOE levels in models, a possible drug target.

Treatments that are working

For the first time, approved drugs slow early Alzheimer's instead of only easing symptoms. The benefit is modest, and they only work for people diagnosed early, which is why the new blood tests matter.

Lecanemab (Leqembi)

FDA approved
Eisai / Biogen · early Alzheimer's · 2023

Clears amyloid and slowed decline by about 27% over 18 months in the CLARITY AD trial. It now comes as an at-home under-the-skin injection for maintenance, and the FDA is reviewing that injection as a starting dose.

Donanemab (Kisunla)

FDA approved
Eli Lilly · early Alzheimer's · 2024

Treatment can stop once amyloid is cleared. In TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 it slowed decline by about 35% in people with lower tau levels. A modified dosing schedule cut brain-swelling side effects.

Trontinemab

Promising · Phase 3
Roche · "brain shuttle" antibody · AAIC July 2026

Designed to cross into the brain more easily. At the high dose, 92% of patients fell below the amyloid threshold by 28 weeks, with relatively low side-effect rates. Phase 3 and the PrevenTRON prevention trial are under way.

Alzheimer's blood tests

FDA cleared
Fujirebio, Roche, C2N · four cleared by Aug 2026

Blood tests for p-tau217 and related markers now help diagnose Alzheimer's without a PET scan or spinal tap. One is cleared for adults as young as 40 with symptoms.

Xanamem

Results due Nov
Actinogen · Phase 2b/3 XanaMIA · 247 patients

An oral pill that lowers excess cortisol in the brain, tested in mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's with high pTau181. The last patient visit was September 22, 2026.

Semaglutide

Missed goal
Novo Nordisk · EVOKE trials · published Mar 2026

In nearly 3,800 people over two years, oral semaglutide (the Ozempic/Wegovy drug) didn't slow early Alzheimer's, despite small changes in spinal-fluid markers.

How other countries treat Alzheimer's

Most wealthy countries have now approved the amyloid drugs, but on different terms. Japan and China moved fast, Europe limited who can get donanemab, and the UK approved both but won't fund them on the NHS.

How access differs abroad

Japan

Early and covered
Lecanemab Sept 2023 · donanemab Sept 2024

Japan approved lecanemab within months of the U.S. and covers it under national insurance. A survey of 311 specialists after the first year points to infrastructure, not approval, as the main bottleneck.

PubMed Central

United Kingdom

Approved, not funded
NICE final guidance · 2025

The UK regulator approved both drugs, but NICE recommended against routine NHS use on cost grounds. Patients mostly can only get them privately. Approved isn't the same as available.

European Union

With limits
Lecanemab Apr 2025 · donanemab Sept 2025

The EU approved donanemab only after a re-examination, and only for people who don't carry two copies of APOE4, the group at highest risk of brain swelling.

How it changed over time
  1. 1990sU.S. and Europe approve the first symptom drugs (cholinesterase inhibitors), followed by memantine.
  2. 2023U.S. and Japan approve lecanemab, the first drug shown to slow the disease.
  3. 2024China, South Korea and the UK approve lecanemab. The U.S. and Japan approve donanemab.
  4. 2025EU approves lecanemab (April) and donanemab (September, limited). Australia approves both after first refusing lecanemab. Canada approves lecanemab. The UK's NICE declines NHS funding. The U.S. clears its first Alzheimer's blood test.
  5. 2026Canada approves donanemab. The U.S. reaches four cleared blood tests. Trontinemab moves into Phase 3 prevention.

Natural remedies & supplements

No supplement prevents or cures Alzheimer's. Lifestyle has stronger evidence than any pill on a store shelf. We rate each by the strength of human evidence.

Good human evidence Observational / mixed Tested, did not work, or possible harm

For lowering risk

Structured lifestyle program

U.S. POINTER (JAMA, 2025): regular exercise, the MIND diet, mental and social challenge, and health checks protected thinking skills over two years compared with a self-guided program.

Treat hearing loss, blood pressure & LDL

Among the 14 risk factors the 2024 Lancet Commission links to about 45% of dementia cases. Hearing aids, blood-pressure control and cholesterol care are low-risk steps.

MIND / Mediterranean diet

Leafy greens, berries, nuts, olive oil and fish are linked to slower cognitive aging, and the diet is part of the POINTER program.

Supplements people ask about

Low-dose lithium

Promising lab results, including 2026 data on tau. There's no human proof yet. Lithium can harm the kidneys and thyroid, so don't take it on your own.

Omega-3 (DHA) supplements

Controlled trials in people who already have Alzheimer's didn't slow decline. Fish in the diet is still reasonable.

Ginkgo biloba

A large prevention trial in older adults found it didn't reduce dementia.

Glucosamine

A September 2026 health-record study linked it to faster progression from mild impairment to dementia. Ask your doctor if you take it.

Be careful: "memory" supplements are sold with big claims and little evidence. Some common medicines, such as strong anticholinergic sleep and allergy pills, can worsen confusion in older adults. Review every pill and supplement with the care team.
