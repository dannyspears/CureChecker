Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD). It is caused by changes in the DMD gene that leave muscles without dystrophin, a protein that protects muscle fibers. Steroids (deflazacort, prednisone or vamorolone) and givinostat slow the loss of strength. Four exon-skipping drugs are approved in the U.S. for certain gene changes, but only under accelerated approval. Elevidys, a one-time gene therapy, makes a shortened dystrophin. Since November 14, 2025 it carries a boxed warning for serious liver injury and is limited to people 4 and older who can still walk. Our view: today's treatments slow Duchenne, they do not stop it, and several new drugs face FDA decisions in the next five months.

  1. Gene therapy, now with limits.After two deaths from acute liver failure in people who could no longer walk, the FDA added a boxed warning to Elevidys on November 14, 2025 and removed the non-walking group from its label.
  2. Exon-skipping evidence is still thin.The ESSENCE trial of casimersen and golodirsen missed its main goal in November 2025. The FDA is reviewing a request to give them full approval anyway, with a decision due February 28, 2027.
  3. Not every gene therapy works.Pfizer's gene therapy, fordadistrogene movaparvovec, did no better than placebo in its Phase 3 CIFFREO trial (published March 2026), and Pfizer stopped developing it.
  4. More FDA decisions ahead.Deramiocel (due November 22, 2026), z-rostudirsen for exon 51 (due January 21, 2027) and del-zota for exon 44 (accepted September 2026) are all under FDA review.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Care decisions belong with a Duchenne-experienced neuromuscular team.

Deramiocel: HOPE-3 two-year extension data

Capricor reported that in the open-label extension, people who switched from placebo to deramiocel lost 0.49 points of arm function in their first treated year versus 2.05 points on placebo. Of 106 randomized, 98 joined the extension. These are company-reported, descriptive results without statistical testing. The FDA decision date is still November 22, 2026.

Treatments that are working

Steroids are the base of care. Newer drugs and a gene therapy add to them for some people. Heart, lung and physical care protect the body as the disease moves on.

Steroids: deflazacort & prednisone

Standard
Emflaza approved Feb 9, 2017 · ages 2 and older

Daily steroids slow the loss of muscle strength. Long-term use can weaken bones and slow growth. Steroids must never be stopped suddenly.

Agamree (vamorolone)

FDA approved
Approved Oct 26, 2023 · ages 2 and older

A steroid-like drug. Studies suggest it works about as well as prednisone with less effect on bone and growth.

Duvyzat (givinostat)

FDA approved
Approved Mar 21, 2024 · ages 6 and older

The first non-steroid pill for all gene types. In an 18-month trial in boys also taking steroids, it slowed the decline in four-stair-climb time compared with placebo. Watch for low platelets and high triglycerides.

Elevidys gene therapy

Approved · restricted
One-time infusion · ages 4+ who can walk · boxed warning

Makes a shortened "micro-dystrophin". Its main Phase 3 trial (EMBARK) missed its main goal at one year, but the FDA gave full approval for walking patients in 2024 based on other measures. It can cause serious, sometimes fatal liver injury.

Exon-skipping drugs

Accelerated approval
Exondys 51 · Vyondys 53 · Viltepso · Amondys 45

Weekly infusions for specific gene changes (exons 51, 53 or 45). They raise dystrophin a little. Benefit on strength has not yet been confirmed: ESSENCE and RACER53 missed their main goals.

Heart, lung & physical care

Standard
Neuromuscular team · cardiology · pulmonology · therapy

Regular heart and breathing checks, heart medicines, breathing support, stretching and physical therapy are part of the international care guidelines.

Natural remedies & daily care

No supplement replaces dystrophin or stops Duchenne. Daily care that protects muscles, bones, heart and lungs matters most. We rate each by the strength of evidence.

Strong expert consensus Limited / mixed evidence No evidence, or possible harm

Helpful daily care

Stretching & physical therapy

Care guidelines recommend regular stretching and supervised therapy to keep joints flexible.

Vaccines, including yearly flu shots

PPMD advises keeping vaccines up to date and getting a flu shot every year. People on daily steroids should avoid live vaccines when possible.

Creatine

A 2013 Cochrane review found short-term creatine raised muscle strength in muscular dystrophies. Ask the care team first.

Promoted without evidence, or risky

Unapproved stem cell clinics

The FDA warns these products are unapproved and have caused serious harm, including infections and blindness.

Stopping steroids suddenly

This can cause a life-threatening adrenal crisis. Steroids must be lowered slowly under medical care.

"Cure" supplements

No supplement has been shown to restore dystrophin or stop Duchenne.

Be careful: people with Duchenne should never receive the anesthesia drug succinylcholine, which can cause dangerous potassium levels. Anyone on daily steroids needs stress-dose steroids for surgery or serious illness. Tell every emergency and surgical team about Duchenne and the steroids.

What causes Duchenne muscular dystrophy

Duchenne happens when a change in the DMD gene on the X chromosome stops the body from making dystrophin. Without it, muscle fibers are damaged each time they work.

Step 1 · The gene

The DMD gene is changed

~1 in 3,300boys
  • X-linked: boys have one X chromosome, so one changed copy causes the disease
  • Often passed down: from a mother who carries the change, though some cases are new
  • Becker cousin: a milder form, Becker, comes from changes in the same gene
Step 2 · The tipping point

No dystrophin is made

  • Dystrophin helps hold muscle fibers together and protects them as they contract
  • It is needed in the muscles that move the body and in the heart
Step 3 · The damage

Muscle is slowly lost

  • Early childhood: signs usually appear between ages 3 and 6
  • Childhood: muscle weakness keeps growing
  • Teen years: most need a wheelchair by adolescence
  • Heart: heart-muscle disease (cardiomyopathy) usually begins in the teens
Step 4 · The result

Heart and breathing risks

  • Weak breathing and heart muscles can cause life-threatening problems in a person's 20s or 30s
  • Steroids, heart medicines and breathing support help people live longer
The Becker clue: people with Becker muscular dystrophy make a shorter but partly working dystrophin and have a milder disease. Exon skipping and micro-dystrophin gene therapy both try to copy this.
Five years after gene therapy (2026): all 4 boys in the first Elevidys study were still walking five years later. That is a very small study without a placebo group.
Steroids and growth (2026): a 2026 study found that prednisone, but not vamorolone, lowered blood markers of bone and cartilage growth in young boys with Duchenne.
