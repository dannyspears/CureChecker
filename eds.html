Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for any type of Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (EDS). EDS is a group of 13 inherited conditions that affect connective tissue, and they differ a lot. The one with the most research progress is vascular EDS (vEDS), where a beta-blocker called celiprolol is in a Phase 3 U.S. trial. Most other types are managed with physical therapy, joint protection and pain care. Our view: gene-level fixes are far off, and the nearest gains are better diagnosis, a proven vEDS drug, and clearer care pathways.

  1. vEDS has one promising drug.In a 2010 French trial, celiprolol cut the rate of arterial ruptures and tears by about two-thirds. The U.S. Phase 3 DiSCOVER trial is testing it in about 150 people with COL3A1-confirmed vEDS. The FDA gave it Breakthrough Therapy designation.
  2. A second drug may add to it.In the ARCADE trial of 57 adults already taking celiprolol, adding irbesartan reduced severe arterial events by about 50% (Circulation, 2025). It is a small study, so confirmation is needed.
  3. New diagnostic rules are coming.The Ehlers-Danlos Society will publish an updated classification on December 2, 2026, with a clinician diagnostic guide planned for March 2027. The rules for the common hypermobile type may change.
  4. No test or drug yet for the common hypermobile type.No gene has been found for most people diagnosed with hypermobile EDS, so diagnosis is clinical, and care is therapy-based.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Care decisions belong with a doctor experienced in EDS.

Treatments that are working

No drug is approved for any type of EDS. Care aims to protect joints, manage pain, and, in vascular EDS, reduce the risk of artery rupture.

Celiprolol (vEDS)

Phase 3
DiSCOVER trial · Zevra Therapeutics · ~150 patients

A beta-blocker that also relaxes blood vessels. A 2010 French trial found about a two-thirds lower rate of arterial events. The FDA has granted Breakthrough Therapy and orphan designations. A 2026 study of the first non-European cohort found it generally well tolerated.

Irbesartan add-on (vEDS)

Small trial
ARCADE trial · 57 adults · Circulation, 2025

Adding irbesartan to celiprolol roughly halved severe arterial events over two years, a promising result that needs confirmation in a larger study.

Physical therapy

Standard
Hypermobile and other types

Strengthening exercises that stabilize loose joints are the core of care for hEDS. A feasibility study of an exercise and lifestyle program for adults with vEDS is also registered.

Pain management

Standard
Multidisciplinary pain care

Pacing, bracing, heat, and carefully chosen medicines. Because many people have chronic pain, specialist pain clinics are often part of care.

Surgical care

Case by case
Orthopedic and vascular

Tissue is fragile, so surgery is approached with caution, especially in vEDS, and is done by teams who know the condition.

Genetic testing & counseling

Essential
All suspected types

A confirmed gene change identifies the type and, for vEDS, guides life-saving monitoring. hEDS has no gene test yet.

Natural remedies & daily care

No supplement fixes faulty collagen. Daily habits that protect joints and manage symptoms help most. We rate each by the strength of evidence.

Helpful daily care

Guided strengthening exercise

Supervised, low-impact strengthening helps stabilize loose joints.

Pacing & joint protection

Splints, bracing and avoiding end-range stretching are widely used by therapists.

Sun protection & skin care

Gentle handling and protecting fragile skin reduces tears and bruising.

Promoted without evidence, or risky

Collagen and vitamin C supplements

No evidence they fix genetic collagen faults.

Aggressive stretching or manipulation

Can injure loose joints. Seek therapists who know hypermobility.

Fluoroquinolone antibiotics

FDA safety communications link this class to aortic aneurysm and dissection. People with vascular EDS should discuss this with their doctor.

Be careful: people with vascular EDS should carry an emergency card, know their type, and seek emergency care for sudden severe pain in the chest, abdomen, head or neck.

What causes Ehlers-Danlos syndrome

EDS is a family of inherited conditions in which collagen and related proteins, the "glue" and scaffolding of skin, joints, blood vessels and organs, are faulty. There are 13 recognized types. Genes are known for 12 of them. The most common, hypermobile EDS, has no known gene.

Step 1 · The gene

Many genes, many types

50% chancefor most dominant types
  • Hypermobile (hEDS): most common, cause unknown
  • Classical: COL5A1 or COL5A2
  • Vascular (vEDS): COL3A1, the most dangerous type
  • Some types are recessive, needing a change in both gene copies
Step 2 · The tipping point

Connective tissue is weak

  • Collagen gives skin, joints, vessels and organs strength
  • Faulty or too little collagen makes tissue stretchy or fragile
Step 3 · The damage

Depends on the type

  • Joints: loose, easily dislocated, often painful
  • Skin: stretchy, fragile, slow to heal, wide scars
  • Vessels: thin walls in vascular EDS
  • Other: fatigue, digestive and heart-rate issues are common in hEDS
Step 4 · The result

Pain, injury, or emergency

  • Most types cause chronic pain and injury risk
  • vEDS can cause sudden artery or organ rupture, a medical emergency
Not one disease: EDS types differ in genes, symptoms and risk. A treatment for one type may not apply to another, and "hypermobility" alone doesn't mean EDS.
Diagnosis is changing: the Ehlers-Danlos Society's "Road to 2026" project is revising classification and criteria for EDS and hypermobility spectrum disorders, with publication due Dec 2, 2026.
Biomarker hints (2025): a study found a common pattern of extracellular-matrix fragments in the blood plasma of people with hEDS and hypermobility spectrum disorders, a possible future test. It is early research.
