Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for glioblastoma. Standard treatment of surgery, radiation and temozolomide gives a median survival of about 15 months, and only about 7% of people live 5 years. That has barely changed in 20 years. Our view: progress will come from getting drugs and immune cells past the blood-brain barrier, and from combining treatments matched to each tumor.

  1. Opening the blood-brain barrier: a big early signal.Focused ultrasound briefly opens the barrier so chemo can get in. In a 2025 Lancet Oncology study, median survival was 31 months, compared with 19 in matched patients. It was a small, non-randomized trial.
  2. CAR-T cells given into the brain's fluid: real but brief responses.Two-target CAR-T shrank tumors fast. In updated 2026 results, 28% of patients were alive beyond 18 months. Making the responses last is the next challenge.
  3. Vaccines and viruses: approved in some places.Japan conditionally approved Delytact, a cancer-killing virus, in 2021. The DCVax-L vaccine has been under UK review since late 2023.
  4. Matching treatment to the tumor: the long game.MGMT status already predicts who benefits from temozolomide. New drugs target specific changes, such as EGFRvIII.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Treatment decisions belong with a neuro-oncology team, ideally at a center that treats many brain tumors.

What causes glioblastoma

Glioblastoma is the most common and most aggressive primary brain cancer in adults. It grows from glial cells, the cells that support neurons. For almost everyone it isn't inherited and has no known cause. Random DNA changes build up until a cell grows out of control.

Step 1 · The hits

Mostly random

  • Age: most common in people in their 60s and 70s
  • Radiation: earlier high-dose radiation to the head is the only clear environmental cause
  • Rare syndromes: Li-Fraumeni, neurofibromatosis type 1 and Lynch syndrome
  • Not proven: cell phones, diet and head injury
Step 2 · The tipping point

Growth switches stick "on"

  • Common changes include EGFR amplification, PTEN loss, TERT promoter mutations and CDKN2A loss
  • By definition (WHO 2021), glioblastoma is IDH-wildtype. IDH-mutant tumors are a different disease.
Step 3 · The damage

Why it's so hard to treat

  • Infiltrates: tendrils spread into healthy brain, so surgery can't remove every cell
  • Blood-brain barrier: keeps most drugs out
  • Immune "cold": the tumor switches off nearby immune cells
  • Varied: different cells within one tumor resist different drugs
Step 4 · The result

It nearly always returns

  • Headaches, seizures, and changes in speech, vision or personality
  • Median survival is about 15 months, and about 7% live 5 years
  • MGMT-methylated tumors respond better to temozolomide
MGMT status matters: a DNA repair gene decides how well temozolomide works. If it's switched off ("methylated"), the chemo is more effective. Several 2026 trials target the harder "unmethylated" group.
Telling regrowth from treatment effects (Sept 2026): the FDA approved Pixclara, a PET scan that helps doctors tell real tumor regrowth from radiation damage, a common source of confusion.
Getting past the barrier: focused ultrasound, nasal delivery (NEO100) and direct infusion into brain fluid are all being tested to get drugs where they're needed.

Treatments that are working

Standard care has changed little since 2005. The most encouraging new results come from opening the blood-brain barrier and from immune therapies placed directly into the brain. Most are still small, early trials.

Focused ultrasound + temozolomide

Promising · Phase 1/2
Insightec · BT008NA trial · The Lancet Oncology

Microbubbles plus focused ultrasound open the blood-brain barrier before each chemo dose. Median survival was 31.3 months, versus about 19 in 185 matched patients. It wasn't randomized, so a larger trial is needed.

Two-target CAR-T (EGFR + IL13Rα2)

Early signal · Phase 1
Penn Medicine · recurrent GBM · ASCO Post Sept 2026

Given into the brain's fluid. With longer follow-up there was no delayed toxicity, median survival was 12 months, 5 of 18 patients lived beyond 18 months, and one still had a response at 33 months.

Tumor Treating Fields (Optune)

Approved
Novocure · newly diagnosed and recurrent GBM

A wearable device sends electric fields through the scalp to disrupt dividing cancer cells. Added to temozolomide, it extended survival in a Phase 3 trial. It's approved in the U.S., EU, Japan and China, but not routinely funded on the UK's NHS.

DCVax-L

Under UK review
Northwest Bio · personalized dendritic-cell vaccine

Made from the patient's own immune cells and tumor. The company reports longer survival compared with outside controls, a method that's been debated. The UK MHRA application has been under review since December 2023.

Delytact (teserpaturev)

Approved · Japan
Daiichi Sankyo · cancer-killing virus · 2021

A modified herpes virus injected into the tumor. Japan gave it conditional, time-limited approval for malignant glioma. It was the world's first virus therapy approved for brain cancer.

Ultra-short radiation schedule

Missed goal
Netherlands · randomized Phase 3 · 2026

Six radiation sessions instead of 30 didn't prove as good as standard care. Median survival was about 13 months, versus about 21. Standard-length radiation remains the norm.

How other countries treat glioblastoma

Surgery, radiation and temozolomide are standard everywhere. The differences are in the extras. Japan approved a cancer-killing virus. The UK is reviewing a personalized vaccine. Tumor Treating Fields are routine in some countries and hard to get in others.

Natural remedies & supplements

No supplement or diet cures glioblastoma. A few are being studied alongside standard care, and some can interfere with chemo or surgery. We rate each by the strength of human evidence.

Good human evidence Observational / mixed Lab only, or possible harm

Claims you'll see online

Ketogenic diet

Small trials show it's feasible alongside treatment, but there's no proof it extends survival. Only try it with a dietitian, because weight loss is risky.

Cannabis / cannabinoids

Lab studies are interesting, and small early trials have been run. It's not shown to control the tumor, but it may help appetite, nausea and sleep.

Boswellia (frankincense)

A small trial found less brain swelling after radiation. It isn't a tumor treatment. Ask before combining it with steroids.

Use with caution

High-dose antioxidants

Might blunt how radiation and chemo kill tumor cells. Skip large doses during treatment unless your team agrees.

St. John's wort and other herbs

Can change how seizure medicines and chemo are processed. Always check with your pharmacist.

Exercise & rehab

Helps fatigue, strength and mood during and after treatment. Ask about seizure-safe activities.

Be careful: clinics abroad selling unproven "cures" for glioblastoma can be very costly and may delay treatment that works. Bring anything you're considering to your neuro-oncology team, and use the registries above to find real trials.
