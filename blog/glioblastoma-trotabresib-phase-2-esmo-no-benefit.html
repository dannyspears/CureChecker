Glioblastoma: Trotabresib Did Not Help in a Phase 2 Trial

In short: A randomized Phase 2 trial of 184 people with newly diagnosed glioblastoma found that adding the drug trotabresib to standard radiation and temozolomide did not help. Median time without tumor growth was 7.9 months with trotabresib and 8.2 months with standard care, and median survival was 16.9 versus 16.7 months. Severe low platelet counts were more common with trotabresib. Trotabresib is not an approved treatment, and glioblastoma has no cure.

What was tested

The trial (NCT04324840) asked whether adding trotabresib to the usual first treatment for glioblastoma, radiation plus the chemotherapy temozolomide, would help people with newly diagnosed IDH-wildtype tumors. It was a randomized Phase 2 trial. Patients were assigned 2 to 1 to receive trotabresib or standard care alone.

The results were presented by Dr. Angelo Dipasquale of IRCCS Humanitas Research Hospital in Milan at the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) Congress in Madrid, October 23-27, 2026. We read the report from Cancer Network, a trade publication, not a peer-reviewed paper.

What the results showed

The trial included 184 people. Median progression-free survival, the time before the tumor grew again, was 7.9 months with trotabresib and 8.2 months with standard care alone. At 12 months, 22.2% of people on trotabresib and 38.9% on standard care had no tumor growth.

Median overall survival was 16.9 months with trotabresib and 16.7 months with standard care. The report calls this difference not meaningful (p = .898).

Side effects

Severe (grade 3 or 4) low platelet counts happened in 37.0% of people on trotabresib and 15.3% on standard care. Severe low neutrophil counts were 16.8% versus 8.5%.

Doses had to be lowered in 43.7% of people on trotabresib versus 18.6% on standard care, and doses were paused in 49.6% versus 28.8%. About the same share of people stopped treatment in each group (5.9% versus 5.1%).

What the researchers said

The researchers concluded trotabresib did not improve progression-free or overall survival. Dr. Dipasquale said the late separation of the survival curves suggested that people received different amounts of therapy, and that dose and safety questions in early glioblastoma trials deserve more attention.

What this means

This is a negative result for one drug in one trial. It does not change standard care. Trotabresib is not approved for glioblastoma, and glioblastoma has no cure. Our glioblastoma report lists other treatments and trials in progress.

Frequently asked questions

Did trotabresib work for glioblastoma?

Not in this Phase 2 trial. Adding it to radiation and temozolomide did not lengthen time without tumor growth or overall survival.

Is trotabresib approved?

No. It is an experimental drug and is not approved for glioblastoma.

Is there a cure for glioblastoma?

No. Standard care is surgery, radiation and temozolomide, and researchers are still testing new approaches.
