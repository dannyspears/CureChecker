Duchenne Exon 44 Drug Del-zota: Phase 1/2 Results Published

In short: Peer-reviewed Phase 1/2 results for del-zota, an experimental Duchenne drug for people with exon 44 skipping-amenable gene changes, appeared in The Lancet Neurology. The drug raised dystrophin and had an acceptable safety profile in a small trial. It is not approved, and Duchenne has no cure.

What was published

The Lancet Neurology published results from EXPLORE44, a Phase 1/2 trial of delpacibart zotadirsen (del-zota). The paper was added to PubMed on October 14, 2026, and its print issue is dated November 2026.

Del-zota is for Duchenne muscular dystrophy caused by gene changes that can be treated by skipping exon 44 of the DMD gene. The paper says no exon-skipping treatment is approved for this group. It is an antibody linked to a molecule that skips exon 44, and it is designed to deliver that molecule to muscle.

What the trial found

Part B of the trial included 26 boys and men with Duchenne aged 7 to 27. They were randomly assigned to del-zota or placebo and received three infusions. Neither they nor the staff knew who got the drug. The trial also tested 45 healthy adult volunteers in Part A.

  • Exon 44 skipping rose by an average of 36.6% at 5 mg/kg and 43.4% at 10 mg/kg, versus 1.6% on placebo.
  • Dystrophin levels rose by an average of 25.0% at 5 mg/kg and 25.6% at 10 mg/kg, versus a 0.7% drop on placebo.
  • Most side effects were mild or moderate. Two people on the 5 mg/kg dose stopped treatment because of side effects, one with an anaphylactic reaction and one with an infusion-related reaction.
  • The authors also report lower levels of markers of muscle damage.

How to read these results

This was a small, early trial built mainly to test safety. Part B had 19 people on del-zota and 7 on placebo. The measured outcomes were exon skipping and dystrophin, not walking or strength. A higher dystrophin level is a lab result, and the paper does not show that people moved or felt better. The authors say the findings support a larger Phase 3 study to look at benefit and long-term safety.

The trial was sponsored by Avidity Biosciences, now part of Novartis.

Where del-zota stands

Del-zota is not approved. Our Duchenne report lists a September 2026 report from patient groups that the FDA accepted the company's application for accelerated approval with priority review, and that a Phase 3 confirmatory trial called SAFARI44 has started. Acceptance for review is not approval, and the FDA may or may not approve it.

Frequently asked questions

Is del-zota approved for Duchenne?

No. As of October 16, 2026, our report lists it as under FDA review for accelerated approval. Acceptance for review is not approval.

Does del-zota help boys with Duchenne move better?

This trial did not measure that. It showed more exon skipping and more dystrophin, and the authors say a larger Phase 3 study is needed to look at benefit.

Is there a cure for Duchenne muscular dystrophy?

No. Treatments aim to slow the disease or replace missing dystrophin, but there is no cure today.
