01

What causes ALS

ALS is a "multi-hit" disease. Genes, environment, and age add up until motor neurons cross a tipping point. In almost every case, that tipping point involves one protein: TDP-43.

Step 1 · The hits

Risk adds up

~10–15% inherited~85–90% sporadic
  • Genes: C9orf72, SOD1, TARDBP, FUS and 40+ others
  • Environment: pesticides, heavy metals, smoking, military service
  • Age: most diagnoses come between 55 and 75
Step 2 · The tipping point

TDP-43 leaves the nucleus and clumps

  • Seen in about 97% of all ALS cases, inherited or not
  • Lost from the nucleus, it can no longer edit the cell's RNA messages
Step 3 · The damage

Five things go wrong at once

  • Bad RNA edits: repair genes like STMN2 and UNC13A get garbled
  • Clean-up fails: the cell's protein recycling (autophagy) slows
  • Energy drops: mitochondria falter
  • Over-excitation: too much glutamate signalling
  • Immune attack: microglia start removing motor neurons
Step 4 · The result

Motor neurons die

  • Nerve signals stop reaching muscles
  • Weakness spreads to limbs, speech, swallowing and breathing
  • Average survival 2–5 years after symptoms begin
Newest clue (Aug 2026): Salk Institute scientists showed microglia use "TAM" receptors to tag and destroy motor neurons in ALS mice, pointing at the immune system as an active driver.
Environment (Aug 2026): a meta-analysis of 11 studies found on-the-job pesticide exposure linked to about 60% higher ALS risk, nearly 3× at high exposure.
Clean-up failure (Feb 2026): motor neurons lose chaperone-mediated autophagy, a selective protein-recycling system they depend on more than other cells.
02

Treatments that are working

The clearest wins come from drugs that silence a single faulty gene. These help a minority of patients with that gene, but the benefit is the largest ALS has ever seen.

Ulefnersen

Phase 3 win
Otsuka / Ionis · FUS-ALS · announced Sept 22, 2026

The FUSION trial met its main goal (p = 0.0005), improving function and survival together over 72 weeks. It is the first placebo-controlled proof that targeting the cause of a genetic ALS works. FDA filing planned; early access program being set up.

Tofersen (Qalsody)

FDA approved
Biogen / Ionis · SOD1-ALS · long-term data Dec 2025

After 3–5 years on the drug, about 1 in 4 people stabilized or improved in strength and breathing, and survival reached nearly 5 years versus a typical 2–3. Doctors called that "unheard of" in SOD1-ALS.

AP-101

Promising · Phase 2
AL-S Pharma · sporadic and SOD1-ALS · Mar 2026

An antibody against misfolded SOD1. Lowered nerve-damage markers (NfL, pNfH), delayed the need for ventilation, and showed survival benefit in both sporadic ALS and SOD1 carriers. Small trial; needs Phase 3.

PrimeC

Promising · Phase 2
NeuroSense · sporadic ALS · published Mar 2026

A combination of two existing drugs (ciprofloxacin + celecoxib). Slowed decline by 2.23 points on the ALS function scale versus placebo and cut NfL by 9.4%.

CNM-Au8

Seeking approval
Clene · sporadic ALS · NDA planned Q3 2026

Gold nanocrystals that boost nerve-cell energy. FDA said biomarker and survival data may support accelerated approval; confirmatory Phase 3 to start in 2027.

RAG-17

Early signal · Phase 1
Ractigen · SOD1-ALS · Apr 2026

A single dose cut neurofilament light chain, a marker of nerve damage, by 81%, with good safety. Early, but a strong biological signal.

Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Where the cure will come from

There is no cure for ALS today. Our view: the cure will not be one pill. It will be a matched combination, chosen by each person's genetic subtype, started before symptoms spread.

  1. Gene silencing for inherited ALS: nearly there.Tofersen and now ulefnersen show that turning off a toxic gene can halt, and sometimes partly reverse, the disease. Expect the same approach for C9orf72, the most common ALS gene.
  2. Fixing TDP-43 for everyone else: the real cure target.Because TDP-43 fails in ~97% of cases, drugs that restore it or repair the RNA it mis-edits (STMN2, UNC13A) could treat sporadic ALS. ACE-2223 entered human testing this year.
  3. Calming the immune attack: the force multiplier.New microglia findings and trials like COYA 302 and foralumab suggest pairing a root-cause drug with immune control.
  4. Catching it early: the timing that makes it work.Blood tests for neurofilament light can flag ALS months before symptoms in gene carriers. Treating then gives any drug its best chance.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Treatment decisions belong with a neurologist, ideally at a multidisciplinary ALS clinic.

04

Natural remedies & supplements

No supplement prevents or cures ALS. Some have real evidence behind them, many do not. We rate each by the strength of human evidence.

Good human evidence Observational / mixed Tested, did not work

For lowering risk

Omega-3 fats (fish, flax, walnuts)

Large Harvard cohorts linked higher omega-3 intake, especially ALA, to lower ALS risk and slower progression. Food sources are preferred.

Vitamin E (from diet)

Pooled data from about 1 million people tied long-term vitamin E intake to lower risk. Nuts, seeds and leafy greens are good sources.

Fruits, vegetables & carotenoids

Diets rich in antioxidants and carotenoids (orange and dark-green produce) are associated with lower risk and better function.

Avoid pesticides & smoking

Not a supplement, but the best-supported prevention step. Use protective gear if you work with farm or lawn chemicals.

For symptoms and slowing decline

High-calorie diet

Weight loss predicts faster decline. In the LIPCAL-ALS trial, extra calories helped fast-progressing patients live longer. Work with a dietitian.

Ultra-high-dose methylcobalamin (B12)

Japan's JETALS trial slowed decline in early ALS using 50 mg injections twice weekly. Store-bought oral B12 is not the same dose or route.

Vitamin D (if low)

Mixed results. Reasonable to correct a measured deficiency for bone and muscle health.

Cannabis / CBD for spasticity & cramps

Small studies suggest relief of stiffness, cramps, appetite and sleep. Discuss interactions with your care team.

Creatine, CoQ10, lithium

Each was tested in controlled ALS trials and did not slow the disease.

Be careful: unregulated "stem cell" clinics and high-dose supplement stacks can be costly and harmful. Tell your neurologist about everything you take.
