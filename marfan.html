Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for Marfan syndrome, and no medicine approved specifically for it. The main danger is a widening of the aorta, the body's largest artery, which can tear. Today's care is regular imaging, a beta-blocker or a drug called an ARB, and well-timed surgery. Our view: the cure will come from correcting the faulty FBN1 gene early or rebuilding the aortic wall. Until then, finding Marfan early and watching the aorta closely is what saves lives.

  1. ARB drugs slow aortic growth, modestly.In the AIMS trial, irbesartan slowed aortic root growth to 0.53 mm a year versus 0.74 mm on placebo, about 30% less. Losartan lowered growth in the COMPARE trial too (0.77 mm vs 1.35 mm over about three years).
  2. Not better than beta-blockers: a reality check.In the Pediatric Heart Network trial of 608 children and young adults, losartan worked about as well as atenolol, not better. The 2022 U.S. guideline recommends either a beta-blocker or an ARB at the highest dose a patient can tolerate.
  3. Surgery works well when timed right.In one 20-year series, valve-sparing aortic root replacement had early mortality of about 1%, with 91% of patients alive at 10 years and 76% at 20.
  4. Fixing the gene: still in the lab.Base editing has corrected the FBN1 mutation in human cells and embryos, and lab-grown patient cells are revealing new targets. No human gene therapy for Marfan exists yet.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Care decisions belong with a cardiologist and a Marfan-experienced medical team.

What causes Marfan syndrome

Marfan syndrome is caused by a change in the FBN1 gene, which makes fibrillin-1, a protein that gives connective tissue its strength and stretch. Weak connective tissue affects the heart and aorta, eyes, bones and joints.

Step 1 · The gene

One changed copy is enough

50% chancefor each child of a parent with Marfan
  • Common for a rare disease: about 1 in 5,000 people
  • Dominant: a parent with Marfan passes it on half the time. Some cases start as a new change.
  • Related conditions: Loeys-Dietz syndrome and vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome affect the aorta too
Step 2 · The tipping point

Connective tissue is weak

  • Fibrillin-1 forms the "scaffolding" of connective tissue
  • A faulty version also raises a growth signal called TGF-beta, which loosens tissue further
Step 3 · The damage

The aorta stretches

  • Aorta: the root, where the aorta leaves the heart, slowly widens
  • Heart valves: mitral and aortic valves can leak
  • Eyes: the lens can shift, and nearsightedness is common
  • Skeleton: tall height, long limbs and fingers, curved spine, chest wall changes
Step 4 · The result

Dissection risk

  • A widened aorta can tear (dissect) suddenly, which is a medical emergency
  • With monitoring and surgery in time, most of these emergencies can be prevented
The losartan story: a famous 2006 mouse study (Science) showed losartan stopped aortic growth, raising huge hopes. In people the effect has been real but modest, a lesson in how far a lab result can differ from a human trial.
Pediatric research (2026): a June 2026 re-analysis of the Pediatric Heart Network trial used a body-size measure of the aorta to compare losartan and atenolol more fairly.
New targets (Aug 2026): lab-grown blood-vessel cells from people with Marfan show impaired inflammatory resolution, a possible new drug target. This is early, preprint research.

Treatments that are working

No drug is approved specifically for Marfan. Doctors use blood-pressure medicines off-label to slow aortic growth, and surgery to replace the aortic root before it can tear.

Beta-blockers

Standard
Atenolol, propranolol and others · decades of use

Lower heart rate and the force of each beat, easing stress on the aorta. They have the longest track record, including in children and in pregnancy, and are still first-line in many centers.

Irbesartan

Positive trial
AIMS trial · The Lancet, 2019

A double-blind trial in children and young adults found aortic root growth of 0.53 mm a year on irbesartan versus 0.74 mm on placebo, about a 30% slowing, with the drug well tolerated.

Losartan

Modest benefit
COMPARE (adults) · Pediatric Heart Network (608 patients)

Slowed aortic growth in adults (0.77 mm vs 1.35 mm over about three years) and worked about as well as atenolol in children and young adults. Not clearly better than beta-blockers.

Valve-sparing root surgery

Standard
David procedure · prophylactic surgery

Replaces the widened aortic root while keeping the patient's own valve. Surgery is generally advised once the root passes about 50 mm, or about 40 mm with a family history of dissection. In one long series, 91% were alive at 10 years.

Exercise study

Recruiting
Texas Children's Hospital & Stanford · ages 10–25

Studies how moderate exercise affects young people with Marfan, to give clearer activity advice.

Nutritional ketosis

Starting · 2026
Washington University · est. Mar 2026 to Mar 2028

Tests a ketogenic diet as a way to stabilize a chronic aortic dissection in Marfan, a new idea at an early stage.

How other countries treat Marfan syndrome

No country has approved a drug specifically for Marfan, so treatment looks similar worldwide: monitoring, a beta-blocker or ARB used off-label, and surgery. The big trials came from the Netherlands, the UK and the U.S.

Natural remedies & daily care

No supplement strengthens fibrillin or protects the aorta. The most useful daily steps are about managing blood pressure and activity safely. We rate each by the strength of evidence.

Strong expert consensus Observational / mixed No evidence, or possible harm

Protecting the aorta

Keep blood pressure low

Lower pressure means less stress on the aortic wall. Medicines are the main tool, with a low-salt diet and weight management.

Avoid heavy lifting & contact sports

Heavy straining and collisions raise the risk to the aorta. Doctors usually advise avoiding them.

Moderate aerobic exercise

Generally encouraged at a gentle pace, such as walking or swimming. A trial is studying it in young people.

Promoted without evidence, or risky

Collagen, vitamin C & "connective tissue" supplements

No evidence they strengthen fibrillin or slow aortic growth.

Fluoroquinolone antibiotics

FDA safety communications link this antibiotic class to aortic aneurysm and dissection. Tell any prescriber you have Marfan.

Stimulants and decongestants

Can raise blood pressure and heart rate. Ask your doctor before using energy products or decongestants.

Be careful: carry a card or alert saying you have Marfan syndrome, and know the warning signs of aortic dissection: sudden severe chest or back pain. Call emergency services immediately.
