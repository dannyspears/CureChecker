Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for HIV that works for people in general. Daily antiretroviral therapy (ART) keeps the virus under control, and the CDC says people who keep an undetectable viral load will not pass HIV to partners through sex. A small number of people, about ten by April 2026 by one research count, are considered cured or in long-term remission after stem-cell transplants. Those transplants were given to treat life-threatening blood cancers. They are dangerous and are not a treatment for HIV itself. Most of today's progress is about taking medicine less often, for both treatment and prevention. Our view: a cure for everyone will need to clear or permanently silence the virus that hides in resting immune cells. Antibody trials have delayed the virus's return after stopping treatment, but this is still research.

  1. Treatment works, and it prevents spread.The CDC says all people with HIV should take treatment, and an undetectable viral load means HIV will not be passed on through sex.
  2. A new daily pill.On August 27, 2026 the FDA approved Bixlenvo (bictegravir + lenacapavir) for adults whose HIV is already controlled on other medicines.
  3. Longer gaps between doses are coming.A once-weekly pill (islatravir + lenacapavir) met its goals in two Phase 3 trials published in 2026. Phase 3 trials of a twice-yearly treatment with two antibodies began in July 2026. Neither is approved.
  4. Remission is still research.In the RIO trial, 75% of people given two long-acting antibodies had no viral rebound 20 weeks after stopping ART, compared with 11% on placebo. All of them started ART early in infection. Never stop HIV medicine except in a supervised study.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Care decisions belong with an HIV care team.

Treatments that are working

Treatment controls HIV but doesn't cure it. Prevention (PrEP) protects people who don't have HIV. The newest options are about taking medicine less often.

Daily antiretroviral therapy (ART)

Standard
Everyone with HIV · often one pill a day

A combination of HIV medicines. It lowers the virus until tests can't detect it, protects the immune system, and prevents sexual spread. The CDC recommends it for everyone with HIV.

Cabenuva injections

FDA approved
Cabotegravir + rilpivirine · approved Jan 21, 2021

Injections given by a clinician every month or every 2 months instead of daily pills, for people 12 and older whose HIV is already undetectable and who have no resistance to either drug.

Bixlenvo (bictegravir + lenacapavir)

FDA approved
Approved Aug 27, 2026 · adults already suppressed

A once-daily two-drug pill for switching. In two Phase 3 trials (ARTISTRY-1 and ARTISTRY-2) it kept the virus suppressed about as well as the regimens people were already taking.

Once-weekly pill

Phase 3
Islatravir + lenacapavir · ISLEND-1 & ISLEND-2

In two Phase 3 trials, switching to one pill a week worked as well as daily treatment at 48 weeks. The makers plan to apply for approval. It is not approved yet.

Twice-yearly treatment with antibodies

Phase 3
Lenacapavir + teropavimab + zinlirvimab

In a Phase 2 trial, 1 of 53 people had detectable virus at 26 weeks, similar to daily pills. Only people whose virus was highly sensitive to both antibodies could join. Phase 3 trials started July 2026.

Prevention shots (PrEP)

Prevention
Yeztugo every 6 months · Apretude every 2 months

For people who don't have HIV. In PURPOSE 1, none of 2,134 women on twice-yearly lenacapavir got HIV. In PURPOSE 2, 2 of 2,179 participants did. These shots prevent HIV. They don't treat it.

Natural remedies & daily care

No supplement or natural product treats or cures HIV. Some can make HIV medicines work less well. We rate each by the strength of evidence.

Strong expert consensus Limited / mixed evidence No evidence, or possible harm

Helpful daily care

Regular physical activity

HIV.gov strongly encourages it to lower heart-disease risk and help mood and sleep. A Cochrane review found aerobic exercise appears safe and may improve fitness.

Quitting smoking

Smoking's health risks are even higher for people with HIV, and quitting brings major, immediate benefits.

Multivitamins

A Cochrane review found no consistent clinical benefit from routine use, but they make sense when there is a known deficiency or a poor diet.

Promoted without evidence, or risky

St. John's wort

Interacts with some HIV drugs and lowered antiretroviral levels in studies. Avoid it unless your HIV doctor says otherwise.

Garlic, ginkgo, milk thistle supplements

Some forms lowered levels of certain HIV medicines in a systematic review. Ask your pharmacist before taking them.

"Natural HIV cures"

No product cures HIV. Replacing ART with remedies lets the virus come back and damage the immune system.

Be careful: calcium, iron, zinc, vitamin C and multivitamins can lower the levels of some HIV medicines. Timing can matter, so tell your doctor or pharmacist about everything you take, and never stop HIV treatment on your own.

What causes HIV disease

HIV (human immunodeficiency virus) attacks the immune cells that help the body fight infection. The body can't get rid of it on its own. Without treatment it can lead to AIDS, the most advanced stage.

Step 1 · The virus

HIV gets into the body

78% on treatment2025, worldwide
  • How it spreads: mostly through sex without protection or shared drug-injection equipment
  • Worldwide: 41.0 million people were living with HIV in 2025
  • Testing: you can't tell from symptoms, so a test is the only way to know
Step 2 · The tipping point

HIV hides in immune cells

  • HIV infects CD4 cells, a type of white blood cell
  • Some infected cells go quiet. This hidden "reservoir" is why treatment controls HIV but can't clear it
Step 3 · The damage

The immune system weakens

  • Without treatment: CD4 cells are steadily lost
  • With treatment: the virus is kept so low a test can't detect it
  • If ART stops: virus from the reservoir comes back
  • Spread: an undetectable viral load means no spread through sex
Step 4 · The result

AIDS, if untreated

  • AIDS means CD4 cells below 200 per cubic millimeter, or certain serious infections
  • In the U.S., most people with HIV never develop AIDS because treatment stops it
Two kinds of cure (NIAID): scientists separate "remission", where the virus stays suppressed without daily medicine, from "eradication", where HIV is gone from the body completely. Neither is available as a treatment today.
Cures without the "resistance gene" (Dec 1, 2025): most transplant cures used donors with two copies of the CCR5Δ32 mutation, which blocks the usual entry route for HIV. A man in Berlin has stayed in remission more than 6 years off ART after a donor with only one copy, suggesting other factors matter too.
An immune clue (Dec 1, 2025): a Nature study of four antibody trials found that people who later controlled HIV after stopping ART had more "stem-like" CD8 T cells, a lead for future combination treatments.
