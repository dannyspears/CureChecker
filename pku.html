Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for PKU, but it's one of medicine's great prevention stories. Newborn screening plus a strict low-protein diet stops the intellectual disability PKU used to cause. The challenge is that the diet is hard for life. Since 2007, medicines have joined it: sapropterin, pegvaliase and now sepiapterin. Our view: the cure will be a safe gene therapy or gene editing that fixes the liver enzyme for good. Early attempts hit safety problems, so that is still some way off.

  1. Sepiapterin (Sephience): the newest pill.In the Phase 3 APHENITY trial, blood phenylalanine fell by about 63–64%. In follow-up, 97% of people could safely eat more natural protein, with average protein intake up 126%. 43% of people who didn't respond to sapropterin improved.
  2. Pegvaliase (Palynziq): for severe PKU.A daily injection that breaks phenylalanine down. Approved for adults in 2018, and for ages 12 and up in the U.S. in February 2026.
  3. Gene therapy: a setback and a restart.BioMarin's BMN 307 was put on FDA clinical hold in 2021 after liver tumors appeared in mice. Newer approaches such as base editing delivered by fat particles are in early research.
  4. Newborn screening: still the foundation.PKU is caught by a heel-prick test within days of birth in many countries, so treatment starts before any harm is done.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Treatment decisions belong with a metabolic specialist and a dietitian.

What causes PKU

PKU is inherited. A child gets it when both parents pass on a changed copy of the PAH gene. Without a working PAH enzyme, the body can't break down phenylalanine, an amino acid found in all protein, and it builds up in the blood and brain.

Step 1 · The genes

Two changed copies

1 in 4 chancewhen both parents are carriers
  • Recessive: carriers have no symptoms and usually don't know they carry it
  • Many variants: hundreds of PAH changes exist, which is why severity varies
Step 2 · The tipping point

Phenylalanine can't be broken down

  • The PAH enzyme turns phenylalanine (Phe) into tyrosine (Tyr)
  • When it doesn't work, Phe piles up in the blood
Step 3 · The damage

High Phe harms the brain

  • In babies and children: high Phe damages the developing brain
  • Untreated: intellectual disability, seizures and behavior problems
  • In adults: even moderately high Phe can cause trouble with focus, mood and memory
Step 4 · The result

Prevented by early treatment

  • With early treatment, people with PKU usually have normal intelligence
  • The task is keeping Phe in range for life
  • During pregnancy, tight control is essential to protect the baby
Diet is still the foundation: most people with PKU limit natural protein and rely on a special phenylalanine-free formula. New medicines let many eat more normal food.
Medicines work in different ways: sapropterin and sepiapterin boost the enzyme's helper (BH4), while pegvaliase replaces the enzyme's job with a different one.
Access gaps: in the UK, access to special low-protein foods on prescription has been described as "an uneasy path," and food insecurity affects some women with PKU.

Treatments that are working

For more than 60 years the only option was diet. Since 2007, three medicines have been approved, each suited to different people. A cure with gene therapy is still in the lab.

Sepiapterin (Sephience)

Approved
PTC Therapeutics · EU Jun 19, 2025 · FDA Jul 28, 2025 · Japan Dec 22, 2025

A daily BH4 precursor taken by mouth, approved for children and adults across the full range of PKU severity. Blood Phe fell by about 63–64% in the Phase 3 trial, and many people could eat more protein.

Pegvaliase (Palynziq)

Approved
BioMarin · FDA May 24, 2018 · EU 2019 · ages 12+ in the U.S. Feb 2026

A daily injection of an enzyme that breaks down Phe. In the PEGASUS trial in adolescents, Phe fell compared with diet alone. Allergic reactions are a known risk, so it's started under supervision.

Sapropterin (Kuvan)

Approved
BioMarin · FDA Dec 13, 2007 · EU 2008 · Japan 2008

The first PKU medicine. It helps people whose enzyme has some activity, a "BH4-responsive" group. Generic versions have been approved in the U.S., most recently in January 2026.

Diet & medical formula

Foundation
Since the 1960s · all patients

A low-protein diet plus a Phe-free amino-acid formula, started in the first days of life. Still the backbone of care, and essential for people who can't use the medicines.

BMN 307 gene therapy

Clinical hold
BioMarin · AAV gene therapy · hold Sept 2021

The FDA paused the trial after liver tumors were found in 6 of 7 mice given the highest dose, with signs the vector had integrated into DNA. The trial isn't recruiting.

Gene editing

Early research
Base editing with fat-particle delivery

Labs are testing ways to correct the PAH gene directly in liver cells, using adenine base editors and lipid nanoparticles. It's still a research approach.

Blood Phe control worsens with age in Beijing children (JAMA Network Open)

In 325 children with early-treated PKU followed to age 18, average phenylalanine rose above 360 µmol/L from age 6, and only 17 of 78 children (21.8%) with data were in good control by age 12. Children born more recently did better. A single-city, retrospective study.

How other countries treat PKU

Diet is universal. The newest medicine, sepiapterin, reached Europe, the U.S., Japan, Canada, Australia, Switzerland and Brazil within months. Access depends on funding, and many countries have no access to the medicines at all.

Natural remedies & daily care

In PKU, what you eat is the main treatment, so "natural" choices matter a lot. Some popular health foods are dangerous in PKU. We rate each by the strength of evidence.

Strong evidence or consensus Observational / mixed No evidence, or harmful in PKU

What works

Low-protein diet with a Phe-free formula

The proven foundation of PKU care. Needs regular blood tests and a metabolic dietitian.

Fruits, vegetables & low-protein specialty foods

Most fruits and vegetables are low in Phe and count little toward the daily limit. Ask your dietitian for specifics.

Checking labels for aspartame

This common sweetener contains phenylalanine, and diet drinks carry a PKU warning.

Be careful with

High-protein supplements and "keto" foods

Whey, collagen and many protein powders are rich in Phe and can push levels dangerously high.

Large neutral amino acids (LNAA)

A prescription-style option for some adults, with mixed evidence. Only use it under a metabolic team's guidance.

Glycomacropeptide (GMP) foods

A low-Phe protein source used in some medical foods. Ask your dietitian whether it suits you.

Be careful: changing your diet or stopping formula without your metabolic team can raise Phe quickly. Don't start any supplement without checking its phenylalanine content first.
