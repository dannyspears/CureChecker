Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). It scars the lungs for reasons no one yet understands, and the scarring is permanent. Three pills are FDA approved: nintedanib (Ofev), pirfenidone (Esbriet) and, since October 7, 2025, nerandomilast (Jascayd). All three slow the loss of lung function. None stops or reverses it. A lung transplant can replace scarred lungs, but it is an option only for some people. Our view: progress now comes from adding treatments together, and an inhaled drug, treprostinil, is under FDA review. A cure would have to stop or undo the scarring, and no treatment has shown that yet.

  1. A third pill.The FDA approved Jascayd on October 7, 2025 for IPF and on December 19, 2025 for progressive pulmonary fibrosis. In its 1,177-person trial, the 18 mg dose slowed lung-function loss by 68.8 mL more than placebo over a year.
  2. An inhaled option is under review.Two Phase 3 trials (TETON-1 and TETON-2) found inhaled treprostinil slowed lung-function loss. The FDA accepted the application, and the company expects a decision in late April 2027.
  3. A survival signal, not yet proof.A July 2026 pooled analysis linked Jascayd 18 mg to a lower risk of death. In the IPF trial alone, the difference in deaths was not statistically clear.
  4. Cheaper copies are coming.The FDA approved the first generic versions of nintedanib on April 2, 2026. Generic pirfenidone has been FDA approved since January 2022.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Care decisions belong with a lung specialist experienced in pulmonary fibrosis.

Sep 2, 2026

FDA accepts the Tyvaso application for IPF

United Therapeutics said the FDA accepted its application for nebulized Tyvaso (treprostinil) in IPF, based on TETON-1 and TETON-2. In the two trials combined, FVC declined 111.8 mL less than with placebo at 52 weeks. The company expects the review to finish in late April 2027. It is not approved for IPF yet.

Deupirfenidone: ELEVATE-IPF Phase 2b

In 257 people, a modified form of pirfenidone at 825 mg three times daily slowed the FVC drop by 91.0 mL compared with placebo over 26 weeks. Stomach side effects were the most common. A larger trial would be needed before approval.

Jascayd and survival: pooled FIBRONEER analysis

Combining the IPF and progressive pulmonary fibrosis trials (average follow-up 16.7 months), the risk of death was 43% lower with 18 mg than with placebo. Death was a secondary endpoint, and several authors work for the drug's maker.

The European Union granted marketing authorization for Jascayd for adults with IPF or progressive pulmonary fibrosis, after a positive opinion from the EMA's committee in May.

May 18, 2026

Inhaled treprostinil: TETON-1 Phase 3

In 598 people, FVC fell 130.1 mL less with treprostinil than with placebo at 52 weeks, and fewer had clinical worsening (31.8% vs 44.5%). There was no clear effect on flare-ups. Cough affected 54.8% vs 33.1%, and 40.5% stopped treatment vs 32.8% on placebo.

May 1, 2026

FIBRONEER-IPF: full follow-up

Over about 15 months, Jascayd did not lower the combined risk of a flare-up, a respiratory hospital stay or death. Deaths were numerically lower with 18 mg, but the difference was not statistically clear. About 13% to 16% of people in each group stopped because of side effects.

Nalbuphine for IPF cough: CORAL Phase 2

In 165 people with chronic cough from IPF, extended-release nalbuphine cut measured cough by 48% to 60% at 6 weeks, compared with 17% on placebo. It treats the cough, not the scarring.

Treatments that are working

Three approved pills slow scarring. Pulmonary rehab, oxygen and, for some people, a lung transplant make up the rest of care.

Jascayd (nerandomilast)

FDA approved
Approved Oct 7, 2025 (IPF) · Dec 19, 2025 (progressive pulmonary fibrosis) · adults

A twice-daily pill that mainly blocks an enzyme called PDE4B. In the FIBRONEER-IPF trial, it slowed the drop in FVC compared with placebo over 52 weeks, alone or with nintedanib or pirfenidone. Diarrhea was the most common side effect. The label's recommended dose is 18 mg twice daily, or 9 mg for people who can't tolerate it.

Ofev (nintedanib)

FDA approved
Approved Oct 15, 2014 · first generics approved Apr 2, 2026

In the two INPULSIS trials (1,066 people), it slowed the yearly drop in FVC by about 94 to 125 mL compared with placebo. Diarrhea affected more than 6 in 10 people taking it, but fewer than 5% stopped because of it.

Esbriet (pirfenidone)

FDA approved
Approved Oct 15, 2014 · generics approved since Jan 2022

In the ASCEND trial, it cut by 47.9% the share of people whose lung function fell sharply or who died. Stomach and skin side effects were more common than with placebo.

Inhaled treprostinil (Tyvaso)

Under FDA review
TETON-1 & TETON-2 Phase 3 · decision expected late April 2027 (company)

A nebulized drug already FDA approved for two forms of pulmonary hypertension. In both trials it slowed the drop in FVC and reduced clinical worsening over 52 weeks. Cough was the most common side effect, and more people stopped treatment than with placebo.

Pulmonary rehabilitation

Standard
Supervised exercise and education

A Cochrane review found rehab probably improves exercise ability, breathlessness and quality of life in interstitial lung disease, with benefits also likely in IPF.

Lung transplant

For some people
Serious IPF · specialist assessment

The only treatment that replaces scarred lungs. It may be an option for some people with serious IPF. The main risks are infection and the body rejecting the new lungs.

Natural remedies & daily care

No supplement has been shown to slow IPF. The daily habits with the best evidence are exercise-based rehab and not smoking. We rate each by the strength of evidence.

Strong expert consensus Limited / mixed evidence No evidence, or possible harm

Helpful daily care

Pulmonary rehabilitation

A Cochrane review found it is safe and probably improves exercise ability, breathlessness and quality of life in lung scarring diseases, including IPF.

Not smoking

Smoking is a common risk factor for IPF. Ask your care team for help quitting.

Oxygen when prescribed

Used to ease breathlessness and help with exercise. A Cochrane review found too few studies to judge oxygen used only during activity.

Promoted without evidence, or risky

N-acetylcysteine (NAC) supplements

In a 264-person trial, NAC did no better than placebo at protecting lung function over 60 weeks.

Prednisone + azathioprine + NAC

This once-common mix led to more deaths and hospital stays than placebo in the PANTHER-IPF trial.

Antacids or reflux surgery to treat IPF

The 2022 international guideline advises against them as a treatment for IPF itself. Reflux symptoms are a separate question for your doctor.

Be careful: breathlessness that suddenly gets much worse can be an acute flare-up of IPF. Get medical care right away. Tell your lung team before starting any supplement or new medicine, because some drugs change how IPF medicines work (Jascayd's label, for example, lists interactions with drugs that affect the liver enzyme CYP3A).

What causes idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis

"Idiopathic" means the cause is unknown. The tissue around the lungs' tiny air sacs becomes thick and stiff and turns into scar tissue. The scarring seems to come from a cycle of damage and healing, but what starts it is not known.

Step 1 · The trigger

The air sacs are injured

Most often diagnosedin the 60s or 70s
  • Age: risk rises as people get older
  • Sex and smoking: more common in men, and smoking is a common risk factor
  • Genes: a parent or sibling with IPF raises risk, and changes in genes such as MUC5B, TERT and TERC have been linked to it
Step 2 · The tipping point

Repair goes wrong

  • Damage and healing repeat, and scar tissue builds up instead of normal lung
  • The tissue around the air sacs becomes thick and stiff
Step 3 · The damage

The lungs stiffen

  • Main symptoms: shortness of breath and cough
  • Early on: some people have no symptoms at first
  • Flare-ups: symptoms can suddenly become severe (an acute exacerbation)
Step 4 · The result

Lung function falls

  • Doctors track forced vital capacity (FVC), how much air you can blow out
  • Current drugs slow the yearly drop in FVC. They do not restore lost lung function
Telomere genes (Mar 29, 2007): researchers found changes in the telomerase genes TERT and TERC in about 8% of families with inherited IPF. These genes protect the ends of chromosomes, which pointed to cell aging as part of the cause.
A common gene variant (Apr 21, 2011): a variant near the MUC5B mucus gene was found in 38% of people with IPF, compared with 9% of people without it.
Suppressing the immune system backfired (May 20, 2012): in the PANTHER-IPF trial, a three-drug mix of prednisone, azathioprine and N-acetylcysteine led to more deaths and hospital stays than placebo, and that part of the trial was stopped.
