Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for male pattern baldness (androgenetic alopecia). It is a common, inherited kind of hair loss: by age 50, more than half of white men show visible signs, according to the American Academy of Dermatology. It is not dangerous to health, but it can matter a lot to the people who have it. In the U.S., two medicines are approved for it: finasteride 1 mg tablets (prescription, approved December 19, 1997) and minoxidil applied to the scalp (over the counter). Neither is a cure. They slow loss or regrow some hair only for as long as they are used. Our view: the newest candidates, such as a hormone-blocking scalp solution called clascoterone, may add options, but none has been approved for hair loss yet.

  4. Popular add-ons have weaker proof.Laser devices are FDA-cleared, and reviews find modest gains. PRP injections and microneedling show promise, but studies are small and vary widely. Saw palmetto, rosemary oil and biotin lack good evidence.

A possible missing link

Please read first. This is an AI-generated hypothesis, written by Cure Checker's AI from published research. It is speculation, not an established finding. It is not a recommendation, not a prediction that a cure exists, and not medical advice. No doctors or scientists have reviewed it, and it may be wrong. Never start, stop, change or delay any treatment because of it. Talk with your dermatologist.

What looks like it is working

  • Blocking the hormone slows the damage. Of 126 Korean men who took finasteride for at least 5 years, 85.7% improved in a look-back study, though some areas thinned again later (J Dermatol, 2019).
  • The stem cells are still there. Bald scalp kept its hair follicle stem cells but lacked the "progenitor" cells they normally turn into to grow a new hair (J Clin Invest, 2011).
  • Wake-up signals work in the lab. In normal mice, raising lactate in follicle stem cells woke them and started a new hair cycle (Nat Cell Biol, 2017). A signal called WNT5A is switched off in stem cells from balding scalp; turning it on slowed hair loss in mice (Adv Sci, 2026). A gel meant to reactivate stem cells is in a larger trial (NCT07819435); its early results are company-reported only.

Where the links are missing

  • Removing one brake was not enough. Prostaglandin D2 is raised in bald scalp and blocks hair growth (Sci Transl Med, 2012), but a pill aimed at this pathway did not regrow hair in 169 men (Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol, 2021). Our inference: stem cells may need both the hormone signal removed and a direct push.
  • A point of no return. Biopsies show inflammation around follicles early and scarring later, sometimes destroying them (J Cosmet Dermatol, 2009). Our inference: there is no simple test to tell which bald patches still have follicles that could recover.
  • Never tried together. The stem-cell gel trial excludes other hair loss treatments (ClinicalTrials.gov). Our inference: we found no trial pairing a stem-cell "wake-up" drug with a hormone blocker, though we may have missed one.

Our hypothesis

Could waking the stem cells that bald scalp still keeps, while the hormone signal is blocked at the same time, grow back full-size hairs that stay, instead of today's partial gains that fade when treatment stops? It could work only where scarring has not destroyed follicles; a test to find those areas does not yet exist.

What would prove it wrong: if follicles woken in bald scalp shrink again within a year or two even with the hormone blocked, or if the pair regrows no more hair than either part alone.

Treatments that are working

Two medicines are FDA-approved for male pattern hair loss. They work only while used. Hair transplant surgery moves hair rather than stopping the process.

Finasteride 1 mg (Propecia)

FDA approved
Approved Dec 19, 1997 · prescription tablet · men only

Lowers DHT, the hormone that shrinks hair follicles. The AAD says it slowed further loss in about 80% to 90% of men. The label lists sexual side effects, including some that continued after stopping, and depression and suicidal thoughts.

Minoxidil on the scalp

FDA approved
Approved Aug 17, 1988 · over the counter since 1996

A liquid or foam. The 5% liquid for men was approved in 1997 and the 5% foam in 2006. The AAD says results can take up to 6 to 12 months to show.

Dutasteride

Approved abroad
South Korea 2009 · Japan · U.S.: prostate only

A stronger DHT blocker. It is approved for male pattern hair loss in South Korea and Japan. In the U.S. it is approved only for an enlarged prostate, so hair use is off-label.

Oral minoxidil

Off-label
Tablets labeled only for severe high blood pressure

In a 2024 trial of 68 men, 5 mg pills were not better than 5% scalp minoxidil overall, and 49% grew unwanted body hair. A purpose-built extended-release version is in Phase 3.

Clascoterone 5% solution

Phase 3 done
Cosmo Pharmaceuticals · U.S. filing planned early 2027

A scalp solution that blocks androgen receptors in the skin. Two Phase 3 trials met their main goal (company-reported). Not approved for hair loss anywhere.

Hair transplant surgery

Established
Moves hair follicles from the back of the scalp

In a 2025 survey of hair restoration surgeons, 85% of men's cases used the FUE method. The AAD calls results permanent and natural-looking, but surgery does not stop further loss.

Natural remedies & supplements

Hair loss products are heavily marketed. None of these is FDA-approved to regrow hair, and most of the studies are small. We rate each by the strength of evidence.

Strong evidence Limited / mixed evidence Weak or no evidence, or possible harm

Some evidence, still limited

Microneedling with minoxidil

A 2025 review of 12 trials (631 people) found more hair with microneedling plus minoxidil than minoxidil alone, but results varied a lot and side effects were more frequent. The British Association of Dermatologists says little research supports it.

Ketoconazole shampoo

A 2020 review found 5 small human studies (318 people) and called it promising as an add-on, but said randomized trials are needed.

Pumpkin seed oil capsules

One 2014 trial in 76 men found more hair than placebo after 24 weeks. One trial is not enough to be sure.

Weak or no evidence

Saw palmetto

The NIH's NCCIH says a few small studies exist and the evidence is too limited to draw conclusions.

Rosemary oil

One 2015 trial (100 people) found it similar to 2% minoxidil, the weaker strength, with no placebo group. Not enough to recommend it.

Biotin

A 2017 review found benefit only in people with an underlying cause of poor hair growth, not in healthy people. High doses can throw off lab tests.

Caffeine shampoos and liquids

One open-label 2017 trial compared a caffeine liquid with 5% minoxidil on a lab measure of growing hairs, not hair counts. Not enough to rely on.

Scalp massage

A 2016 study of 9 healthy men with no comparison group found slightly thicker hairs. It did not study men with pattern baldness.

Be careful: biotin in many hair supplements can cause falsely high or low lab results, including heart tests, so tell your doctor if you take it. The FDA says there is no FDA-approved topical finasteride and has warned about compounded versions. Be wary of "cure" or "regrowth guaranteed" claims: for over-the-counter scalp products, the FDA has ruled that hair-growth claims are unsupported unless the product has gone through approval, as minoxidil has.

What causes male pattern baldness

It comes from inherited genes plus the hormone DHT. In men with the genetic tendency, DHT gradually shrinks hair follicles on the top and front of the scalp, so the hairs they make get thinner and shorter.

Step 1 · The genes

An inherited tendency

More than halfof white men by 50
  • Many genes: a 2018 study of 205,327 men found 624 gene regions linked to it
  • Mother's side counts: a key gene, for the androgen receptor, sits on the X chromosome, which men get from their mother
Step 2 · The tipping point

Testosterone becomes DHT

  • An enzyme, type II 5-alpha reductase, is found in hair follicles and makes DHT
  • Finasteride and dutasteride block this enzyme
Step 3 · The change

Follicles shrink

  • Miniaturization: balding scalp has smaller follicles and more DHT than hairy scalp
  • Thinner hairs: thick hairs are slowly replaced by fine, short ones
Step 4 · The result

A typical pattern

  • Loss is most visible at the temples and the crown
  • Doctors often grade it on the Hamilton-Norwood scale
Genetics (2018): a study of 205,327 men found 624 gene regions linked to male pattern baldness and estimated it is strongly inherited. No single gene decides it.
DHT (FDA label): the enzyme that makes DHT is found in hair follicles. This is why blocking it with finasteride slows hair loss in many men.
Follicle miniaturization (FDA label): balding scalp has shrunken follicles and more DHT than scalp with hair. Treatments aim to stop or partly reverse this shrinking while they are used.
