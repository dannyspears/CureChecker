Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

Pancreatic cancer can be cured mainly when surgery removes it all, and only about 1 in 5 people can have that surgery when they are diagnosed. About 14% of people are alive five years after diagnosis, and about 3% once it has spread. There is still no cure for cancer that has spread. Our view: drugs that switch off RAS, the gene that drives about 9 in 10 cases, are the biggest change in decades. Whether they can raise the cure rate depends on trials now testing them earlier, right after diagnosis and after surgery.

  1. A new kind of RAS blocker is approved.The FDA approved daraxonrasib (Rasonque) on August 26, 2026 for spread cancer after earlier treatment. In the RASolute 302 trial (500 patients), people on the pill lived a median 13.2 months versus 6.7 months on chemo. It doesn't cure the cancer, and rash and mouth sores are common.
  2. Moving RAS drugs earlier: the test that matters for cures.Phase 3 trials are testing daraxonrasib as the first treatment for spread cancer (RASolute 303), after surgery (RASolute 304), and paired with zoldonrasib for KRAS G12D tumors (RASolute 309). Results are still years away.
  3. Personal vaccines after surgery: unproven so far.BioNTech's mRNA vaccine autogene cevumeran is in a 260-patient Phase 2 trial after surgery. A different KRAS vaccine, ELI-002 7P, missed its main goal in June 2026. Vaccines haven't yet been shown to prevent relapse.
  4. Strong chemo after surgery is still the backbone.In the PRODIGE 24 trial, 43% of people given modified FOLFIRINOX after surgery were alive at five years, versus 31% on gemcitabine. Any new drug will be added to this, not replace it.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Treatment decisions belong with an oncologist, ideally at a center that treats many pancreatic cancers. Ask about tumor and genetic testing, which can open up treatment options.

A possible missing link

Please read first. This is an AI-generated hypothesis, written by Cure Checker's AI from published research. It is speculation, not an established finding. It is not a recommendation, not a prediction that a cure exists, and not medical advice. No doctors or scientists have reviewed it, and it may be wrong. Never start, stop, change or delay any treatment because of it. Talk with your oncologist.

What looks like it is working

  • Blocking RAS. In 500 people with spread cancer, the RAS pill daraxonrasib roughly doubled median survival versus chemo (13.2 vs 6.7 months) (NEJM, 2026). It extends life but does not cure.
  • Long-lived immune cells. In a small early trial of a personal mRNA vaccine after surgery, 8 people whose immune systems responded had not reached a median time to relapse after about 3 years, versus 13.4 months in 8 who did not. Their vaccine-made T cells lasted for years (Nature, 2025).
  • Blood tests after surgery. Across 18 studies (965 people), tumor DNA found in the blood after surgery was linked to earlier relapse and shorter survival (Ann Surg, 2026).

Where the links are missing

  • Responses that fade. Resistance to RAS drugs is common, and tumors escape daraxonrasib by different routes depending on their exact KRAS change (Cancer Res, 2026). Our inference: a RAS pill alone may not clear every cancer cell.
  • Immune help, so far only in mice. In mice, an IL-21-like lab molecule turned short responses to KRAS drugs into lasting remissions by working through CD4 T cells (Cell, 2026). Another mouse study paired KRAS drugs with a CTLA-4 immune drug (Nat Commun, 2026). Our inference: no one yet knows whether this happens in people.
  • The right moment. A large trial tests daraxonrasib after surgery and chemo without choosing people by blood test (ClinicalTrials.gov). Another trial uses blood tests to guide chemo after surgery (ClinicalTrials.gov). Our inference: we found no trial pairing a RAS drug with immune help in people whose blood shows leftover cancer after surgery, though we may have missed one.

Our hypothesis

After surgery, leftover cancer is tiny and, by our inference, may not yet resist RAS drugs. Could people whose blood still shows tumor DNA be treated with a RAS drug plus an immune treatment that helps T cells finish the job, and stay free of cancer longer than with a RAS drug alone? The immune partner may be a drug that does not yet exist.

What would prove it wrong: if adding immune help to a RAS drug does not clear blood tumor DNA or delay relapse more than the RAS drug alone in people with leftover disease.

Hypothesis written October 6, 2026. Cure Checker's AI will revisit it when major new evidence appears.

Treatments that are working

For decades, progress in pancreatic cancer came in small steps from stronger chemo combinations. In 2026, a targeted pill aimed at RAS nearly doubled survival in spread cancer that had already been treated.

Daraxonrasib (Rasonque)

FDA approved
Revolution Medicines pill · spread cancer after earlier treatment · Aug 26, 2026

In RASolute 302 (500 patients), median survival was 13.2 months versus 6.7 months on chemo, and tumors shrank in 30% versus 11%. Only 1.2% stopped it because of side effects, versus 11.2% on chemo. Rash and mouth sores are common. It's taken once a day.

NALIRIFOX (Onivyde combination)

FDA approved
Liposomal irinotecan combo · first treatment for spread cancer · Feb 13, 2024

Liposomal irinotecan with oxaliplatin, 5-FU and leucovorin. In NAPOLI 3 (770 patients), median survival was 11.1 months versus 9.2 months with gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel.

Olaparib (Lynparza)

FDA approved
AstraZeneca pill · inherited BRCA changes · Dec 27, 2019

Given after platinum chemo has kept spread cancer in check. In POLO, it doubled the time before the cancer grew (7.4 vs 3.8 months), but overall survival was about the same (18.9 vs 18.1 months). About 5–7% of people with spread disease carry an inherited BRCA change.

Modified FOLFIRINOX after surgery

Standard care
PRODIGE 24 trial · after the tumor is removed

Four-drug chemo after surgery. Median survival was 53.5 months versus 35.5 with gemcitabine, and 43.2% versus 31.4% were alive at five years. It's harder to tolerate, so it's for people who are fit enough.

Autogene cevumeran (mRNA vaccine)

Promising · Phase 2
BioNTech / Genentech · after surgery · trial continuing (Aug 28, 2026)

A vaccine made for each patient's tumor. In a Phase 1 study, the 8 patients whose immune systems responded had not reached a median time to relapse after about 3 years, versus 13.4 months in non-responders. The 260-patient IMcode003 trial is testing it with chemo.

ELI-002 7P (KRAS vaccine)

Missed goal
Elicio · after surgery · June 15, 2026 (company release)

In the 144-patient AMPLIFY-7P trial, it did not significantly extend the time without relapse compared with watching. The company points to benefit in some subgroups, but those were looked at after the fact. A useful negative result.

Natural remedies & supplements

No supplement prevents or cures pancreatic cancer. The best-studied "natural" product, mistletoe, failed in a large trial. What does help is treating the digestion and weight-loss problems this cancer causes. We rate each by the strength of human evidence.

Good human evidence Observational / mixed Lab only, or could cause harm

Claims you'll see online

Mistletoe extract

Tested properly in 290 patients against placebo (MISTRAL). It did not extend survival or improve quality of life, and caused skin reactions at the injection site in 66%.

Curcumin (turmeric)

In a 25-patient trial of 8 g a day, 2 people showed some activity. Very little reached the blood. No controlled trial shows it helps.

High-dose antioxidant stacks

Many chemo drugs work partly through oxidative damage. In a large breast-cancer study (SWOG S0221), antioxidants during chemo were linked to more relapses. No such study exists for pancreatic cancer, so ask your oncologist first.

Ketogenic diet

A 32-patient trial of a medically supervised keto diet with chemo showed trends toward longer survival (13.7 vs 10.2 months), but it was too small to prove anything. Only try it with a medical team.

For recovery and quality of life

Pancreatic enzyme pills (PERT)

About 72% of people with advanced pancreatic cancer can't digest food well. Prescription enzymes with meals were linked to about 3.8 months longer survival in a review of mostly observational studies.

Nutrition support during chemo

In a 60-patient Japanese trial, an easy-to-digest "elemental" drink during chemo helped keep weight and strength, and survival was longer (21.4 vs 12.8 months). One small trial, so it needs confirming. Work with a dietitian.

Ear acupressure for sleep

In an 80-patient trial in Taiwan, 4 weeks of ear acupressure improved sleep and insomnia during chemo. It doesn't treat the cancer.

Vitamin D-like drugs

A prescription vitamin D-like drug (paricalcitol) is being studied with chemo, but it raised calcium in some patients. Store-bought vitamin D is not a treatment.

Be careful: herbal products and high-dose antioxidants can interfere with chemo, and daraxonrasib already causes rash and mouth sores, so check before adding creams or supplements. Clinics selling "natural" or "immune" cancer cures can delay treatment that works. Tell the oncology team about everything you take.

What causes pancreatic cancer

Most pancreatic cancers start in the cells lining the ducts that carry digestive juices. The first step is almost always a broken RAS gene, usually KRAS, which gets stuck in the "on" position and tells cells to keep growing. Over years, more genes break, and the cancer often spreads before it causes symptoms.

Step 1 · The hits

Risk adds up

  • Age: the median age at diagnosis is 71
  • Lifestyle and health: cigarette smoking, obesity, and long-term inflammation of the pancreas (chronic pancreatitis)
  • Inherited genes: a family history, and genes such as BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2 and ATM
Step 2 · The tipping point

KRAS gets stuck "on"

  • RAS mutations are found in more than 90% of pancreatic cancers
  • It is one of the earliest gene changes, and the target of daraxonrasib
Step 3 · The damage

The brakes come off

  • Precancer first: KRAS changes start small growths in the ducts called PanIN
  • More genes break: the "brake" genes CDKN2A, TP53 and SMAD4 are lost as these turn into cancer
  • TP53: altered in about 60–77% of tumors
  • Inherited BRCA changes: the target of the drug olaparib
Step 4 · The result

It spreads before it's found

  • Early cancer causes few symptoms. Later signs include yellow skin (jaundice), belly or back pain, and weight loss
  • About half of people are diagnosed after it has spread
  • 5-year survival is about 44% if it hasn't spread and about 3% if it has
Newest clue (Sept 30, 2026): researchers in Cancer Research showed that tumors escape daraxonrasib in different ways depending on their exact KRAS change. One patient whose cancer escaped this way was helped by a follow-up drug the lab tests predicted.
Making RAS drugs last (Sept 23, 2026): a study in Cell found that in mice, adding an immune signal (an IL-21 mimic) turned short-lived responses to KRAS-blocking drugs into long-lasting remissions. This has not been tested in people yet.
Why it's so common to RAS (May 31, 2026): the RASolute 302 report in the New England Journal of Medicine noted that cancer-causing RAS mutations are present in more than 90% of cases, which is why one RAS drug can help most patients.
