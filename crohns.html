Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for Crohn's disease. It is a long-term disease in which the immune system causes inflammation in the digestive tract, and about 1 million people in the U.S. have it. Medicines and surgery can calm the inflammation and keep people in remission, but they do not cure it. Since 2022 the FDA has approved three IL-23 blockers and an oral pill for adults, and on April 15, 2026 it approved Stelara for children 2 and older. Our view: a cure would need to reset the immune response in the gut, and no treatment does that yet. The progress is in longer-lasting remission and new drug targets such as TL1A.

  1. More options for young children.On April 15, 2026 the FDA approved Stelara (ustekinumab) for moderate to severe Crohn's in children 2 and older. In June, the FDA accepted Takeda's request to approve Entyvio for children 2 and older, with a decision expected in early 2027.
  2. Benefits that last for years.Three-year Omvoh (mirikizumab) data and five-year Tremfya (guselkumab) data, both shown or published in 2026, found that most people who stayed on treatment stayed in remission.
  3. A new target: TL1A.In a Phase 2b trial published in July 2026, 48% of people on the higher dose of duvakitug had a clear improvement on colonoscopy at week 14, compared with 13% on placebo. Phase 3 trials of duvakitug and of tulisokibart are now recruiting.
  4. Not everything works.In March 2026, the Phase 3 ADMIRE CD II trial found that darvadstrocel, a stem-cell treatment for complex fistulas near the anus, did no better than placebo.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Care decisions belong with a gastroenterologist who treats Crohn's disease.

What looks like it is working

  • Strong treatment from diagnosis. In the PROFILE trial, 79% of newly diagnosed adults given infliximab plus an immune-suppressing drug from the start stayed in remission for a year without steroids or surgery, versus 15% with the usual step-up approach (Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol, 2024). Over about 5 years, 6 people treated early needed Crohn's surgery, versus 26 in the step-up group, which the authors say suggests the disease course changed (Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol, 2026).
  • Signs years before symptoms. Blood antibodies against flagellin, a protein on some gut bacteria, were raised up to 10 years before diagnosis in a U.S. military study (Gut, 2026). In healthy relatives of people with Crohn's, antibodies to one piece of flagellin were linked to later getting Crohn's (Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol, 2026).
  • Finding quiet inflammation early. Of 331 healthy relatives screened with a stool test, 9 already had bowel changes that looked like early Crohn's (Gut, 2026).

Where the links are missing

  • Changed disease, or just held back? By year 5, 99% of PROFILE's early-treatment group had received biologic or immune-suppressing drugs (Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol, 2026). Our inference: the trial cannot show whether early treatment reset the immune response or only kept it under control.
  • No test that says the disease has settled. In the SPARE trial, 36% of people in long remission who stopped infliximab relapsed within two years, versus 14% who kept taking it; most regained remission when it was restarted (Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol, 2023). Blood protein tests predicted later relapse only modestly (Gut, 2024). Our inference: no one can yet tell whose immune response has calmed for good.
  • The antibodies may persist. The flagellin-antibody pattern grew stronger after diagnosis (Gut, 2025). Our inference: we found no study testing whether early treatment lowers them, though we may have missed one.

Our hypothesis

Could the immune response to gut-bacteria flagellin, which starts years before symptoms, be part of what keeps Crohn's coming back? If people treated strongly from diagnosis were tracked with a flagellin-antibody blood test (one that does not yet exist for this use), would those whose levels return to normal stay in remission longer after a supervised stop in a clinical trial?

What would prove it wrong: if these antibody levels do not fall with early treatment, or if people whose levels fall relapse just as often after stopping as those whose levels stay high.

Hypothesis written October 6, 2026. Cure Checker's AI will revisit it when major new evidence appears.

Sep 1, 2026

Guselkumab (Tremfya): five-year GALAXI 1 results

In the long-term extension of the Phase 2 GALAXI 1 trial, 97.7% of people still on guselkumab with data at week 240 were in clinical remission and 51.7% had endoscopic remission. Counting everyone who started, 46% were in clinical remission at week 240. No new safety concerns were reported.

Jul 16, 2026

Duvakitug (anti-TL1A): RELIEVE UCCD Phase 2b in Crohn's

In 139 adults, 48% on the 900 mg dose and 26% on 450 mg had an endoscopic response at week 14, compared with 13% on placebo. No safety concerns were found. In February, the companies reported that responses held through 44 more weeks of treatment (company press release). Phase 3 trials (STARSCAPE-1 and -2) are recruiting.

An updated network analysis of 94 randomized trials found high-certainty evidence that ustekinumab brings on remission, and moderate-certainty evidence for adalimumab (alone or with a purine drug) and guselkumab, among others. The authors call for more head-to-head trials and longer safety data.

Treatments that are working

Treatment aims to calm inflammation, prevent flares and keep people in remission. Biologic medicines, a daily pill, older immune-suppressing drugs, nutrition therapy and surgery all play a part.

IL-23 blockers

FDA approved
Skyrizi Jun 16, 2022 · Omvoh Jan 15, 2025 · Tremfya Mar 20, 2025 · adults

Biologics that block the immune signal IL-23. Started by IV infusion or, for Tremfya, by injection under the skin, then continued as home injections. In the Omvoh trial, 53% were in remission at one year, compared with 36% on placebo.

Rinvoq (upadacitinib)

FDA approved
Approved May 18, 2023 · adults · daily pill

A JAK-inhibitor tablet for adults whose Crohn's did not respond to, or who could not take, a TNF blocker. Since October 10, 2025 the label also allows it when TNF blockers are not advisable, after another approved treatment.

Stelara (ustekinumab)

Now for ages 2+
Pediatric approval Apr 15, 2026 · UNITI-Jr trial

Blocks IL-12 and IL-23. Long used in adults, it was approved for children 2 and older in 2026, the first non-TNF biologic for that age group, according to its maker.

TNF blockers

Standard
Infliximab (first approved Aug 24, 1998) · adalimumab

The oldest biologics for Crohn's. A 2026 Cochrane review of 94 trials found that adalimumab, especially with a purine drug such as azathioprine, is among the medicines that bring on remission.

Long-term results

3 to 5 years
VIVID-2 (Omvoh) · GALAXI 1 (Tremfya)

Among people who stayed on Tremfya to week 240 and had data, 97.7% were in clinical remission. Counting everyone who started, 46% were. Omvoh's extension also showed remission held through three years in those who responded in year one.

Surgery & nutrition therapy

Standard
Bowel resection · liquid-formula diet

Surgery treats blockages, fistulas and abscesses but does not cure the disease. For children, an all-liquid formula diet (exclusive enteral nutrition) can be used instead of steroids to bring on remission.

Natural remedies & supplements

No supplement has been shown to cure Crohn's or replace its medicines. Stopping smoking is the strongest step on this list. We rate each by the strength of evidence.

Strong expert consensus Limited / mixed evidence No evidence, or possible harm

Helpful, with some evidence

Stopping smoking

Smoking may double the chance of getting Crohn's, and Crohn's & Colitis UK advises everyone with Crohn's to stop.

Liquid-formula diet for children

A Cochrane review found low-quality evidence that it may work better than steroids for bringing on remission in children. Done only with the care team.

Fasting-mimicking diet

One small, open-label 2026 trial in mild to moderate Crohn's was promising. It needs confirmation and should only be tried with medical supervision.

Unproven

Probiotics

A 2020 Cochrane review found the evidence very uncertain for bringing on remission in Crohn's.

Fish oil (omega-3)

Two large, high-quality trials suggest it probably does not help keep Crohn's in remission. It can cause diarrhea.

Cannabis

A Cochrane review found its effects on Crohn's uncertain, with no firm conclusions on benefit or safety.

Be careful: don't stop or swap prescribed medicines for supplements without talking to your care team. Severe belly pain, vomiting, fever or signs of a blocked bowel need urgent medical care.

What causes Crohn's disease

Doctors aren't sure exactly what causes Crohn's disease. Experts think the immune system overreacts to the normal bacteria in the gut, and that genes, the environment and the gut's microbes all play a part.

Step 1 · The risk

Genes and environment

~1 millionpeople in the U.S.
  • Families: having a parent or sibling with Crohn's raises the chance
  • Smoking: may double the chance of developing it
  • Age: most likely to start between ages 20 and 29, but can start at any age
Step 2 · The tipping point

The immune system attacks

  • The immune system reacts to bacteria that normally live in the intestines
  • Today's medicines block signals that drive this, such as TNF, IL-23 and JAK enzymes
Step 3 · The damage

Inflammation in the gut

  • Where: most often the small intestine and start of the colon, but anywhere from mouth to anus
  • Common symptoms: diarrhea, belly pain and weight loss
  • Other symptoms: tiredness, anemia, fever
  • Outside the gut: joints, skin and eyes
Step 4 · The result

Complications

  • Scarring can narrow or block the bowel, and fistulas and abscesses can form
  • 30% to 55% of people need surgery within 10 years of diagnosis. Surgery helps but does not cure
Clue from genes (May 31, 2001): two studies in Nature linked changes in the NOD2 gene, which helps the body sense bacteria, to a higher chance of Crohn's disease.
Clue from diet (Jan 13, 2026): in a small randomized trial, adults with mild to moderate Crohn's who followed a fasting-mimicking diet 5 days a month had more symptom improvement and lower gut-inflammation markers than those on their usual diet.
Clue from a new target (Jul 16, 2026): a Phase 2b trial showed that blocking TL1A, an immune signal in the gut, improved inflammation seen on colonoscopy. This opens a new route beyond today's drugs.
