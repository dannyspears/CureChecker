Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

About 7 in 10 people whose osteosarcoma has not spread are cured with chemotherapy and surgery, a rate that has barely changed since the 1980s. There is no reliable cure once it spreads or returns. Our view: the next gains will come from drugs that hunt down the cancer cells surgery leaves behind, not from stronger chemo.

  1. Antibody-drug conjugates: the first big win in relapse.Ris-Rez, which carries chemo straight to cells marked with B7-H3, beat standard chemo in a Phase 3 trial in China. GSK holds the rights outside China and has FDA breakthrough status.
  2. Training the immune system after surgery: close to a filing.OST-HER2 was given after lung tumors were removed. Three-year survival was 71% versus 46% in past patients. It was compared with historical records, not a randomized group, so the FDA's view matters.
  3. Adding targeted drugs to first-line chemo: the test that could raise the cure rate.The Children's Oncology Group's AOST2032 trial is adding cabozantinib to standard MAP chemo for newly diagnosed patients. Results are still years away.
  4. Matching treatment to each tumor: the long game.Osteosarcoma's DNA is so scrambled that no single target fits everyone. Trials now grow each patient's tumor in the lab as "organoids" and test drugs on it first.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Treatment decisions belong with an oncologist, ideally at a sarcoma center that treats many bone tumors.

Treatments that are working

The cure rate for osteosarcoma that hasn't spread has stayed flat since the 1980s. The new wins are in relapsed and lung-spread disease, where options were weakest.

Risvutatug rezetecan (Ris-Rez)

Phase 3 win
Hansoh / GSK · relapsed after 2+ treatments · July 30, 2026

In the ARTEMIS-011 trial (117 patients, China), it kept the cancer in check longer than gemcitabine plus docetaxel, and survival trended the same way. It's an antibody that delivers chemo to cells marked with B7-H3. A filing in China is planned, and it has FDA breakthrough status.

OST-HER2

Seeking approval
OS Therapies · after lung tumors are removed · Sept 8, 2026

A weakened, modified Listeria bacterium that trains the immune system to attack HER2. Three-year survival was 71.2% versus 45.8% in past patients (41 treated, p = .002). A rolling FDA application is under way, and the company is aiming for 2027 availability. Not yet tested against a randomized control group.

Cabozantinib

Promising · Phase 3
Exelixis pill · relapsed disease; now first-line trial

In the CABONE trial, 1 in 3 relapsed patients went 6+ months without the cancer growing. Now in AOST2032, which tests whether adding it to standard chemo from day one prevents relapse.

Regorafenib

Guideline option
Bayer pill · relapsed metastatic disease

In SARC024 it doubled the time before the cancer grew (3.6 vs 1.7 months) compared with placebo. It's used off-label in the U.S. as a second-line option. It slows the cancer but doesn't cure it.

Mifamurtide (Mepact)

Approved in Europe
Takeda · non-metastatic, after surgery · EU 2009

An immune stimulant added to chemo after surgery. Europe approved it, but the FDA did not, over concerns about how the trial was analyzed. It's the main approval gap between the U.S. and Europe.

Lenvatinib + chemo

Missed goal
OLIE trial · relapsed disease · published 2024

Adding lenvatinib to ifosfamide and etoposide didn't significantly extend the time before the cancer grew (6.5 vs 5.5 months) in 81 young patients. A useful negative result: not every pill that blocks blood-vessel growth helps.

Natural remedies & supplements

No supplement prevents or cures osteosarcoma. Most "natural" claims come from lab dishes, not patients, and some supplements can interfere with chemo. We rate each by the strength of human evidence.

Good human evidence Observational / mixed Lab only, or could cause harm

Claims you'll see online

Curcumin (turmeric)

Kills osteosarcoma cells in lab dishes and mice. In people, only a small early pilot in advanced osteosarcoma has been run, with no proof of benefit. The body absorbs about 1% of what's swallowed.

Green tea (EGCG) & resveratrol

Lab-only results. Concentrated extracts may interact with chemo, and green tea extract can strain the liver.

High-dose antioxidant stacks

Doxorubicin and cisplatin work partly through oxidative damage. In a large breast-cancer study (SWOG S0221), taking antioxidants before and during chemo was linked to more relapses. Skip them unless your oncologist agrees.

Vitamin D (if low)

Low levels are common in children and teens with cancer. It's reasonable to correct a measured deficiency for bone health. It isn't an anti-cancer treatment.

For recovery and quality of life

Physical therapy & exercise after surgery

Structured rehab is how limb-sparing surgery turns into a working limb. Start early and keep it up.

Nutrition support during chemo

MAP chemo causes nausea and mouth sores, and weight loss is common. Being undernourished is linked to more complications. Work with a dietitian.

Calcium & bone health

Chemo and less activity can thin bones. Get enough calcium from food, plus weight-bearing activity when cleared.

Folic acid around methotrexate

After high-dose methotrexate, your team gives a carefully timed "rescue" of leucovorin (folinic acid). Extra folate from supplements can upset that balance, so ask before taking any.

Be careful: many multivitamins contain folic acid and antioxidants, which can affect how methotrexate rescue, doxorubicin and cisplatin work. Clinics selling "natural" or "immune" cancer cures can delay treatment that works. Tell the oncology team about everything you take.

How it changed over time
  1. 1970sU.S. and Europe: chemo before and after surgery becomes standard. Five-year survival for children climbs from about 40% in 1975 toward 70%.
  2. 1980sThe MAP regimen settles in. Survival for non-spread disease has barely moved since.
  3. 2007U.S.: the FDA declines to approve mifamurtide.
  4. 2009Europe approves mifamurtide, the first new osteosarcoma drug in over 20 years.
  5. 2019U.S.: SARC024 shows regorafenib slows relapsed disease. France's CABONE trial shows the same for cabozantinib in 2020.
  6. 2023U.S., Canada, Australia/NZ: the AOST2032 trial adds cabozantinib to first-line chemo.
  7. 2024Europe-led OLIE trial: lenvatinib + chemo misses its goal.
  8. 2025U.S.: FDA breakthrough status for the B7-H3 drug GSK'227 (Ris-Rez).
  9. 2026China: Ris-Rez wins Phase 3. U.S.: OST-HER2 rolling application, with UK, EU and Australian filings planned.

What causes osteosarcoma

Osteosarcoma is a cancer of the cells that build bone. It strikes most often during the teenage growth spurt, in the fastest-growing bones. There's no single faulty protein to blame. Instead, the tumor's DNA is shattered, and the "guardian" gene TP53 is broken in most cases.

Step 1 · The hits

Risk adds up

  • Growth: peaks at ages 10–19, during the growth spurt. A second, smaller peak comes after 60.
  • Inherited genes: TP53 (Li-Fraumeni), RB1 (retinoblastoma), RECQL4 (Rothmund-Thomson), plus Werner and Bloom syndromes
  • Other: earlier radiation treatment, and Paget disease of bone in older adults
Step 2 · The tipping point

The DNA shatters and TP53 is lost

  • TP53 is altered in about 55% of tumors, and disabled another way (MDM2) in about 5% more
  • Chromosomes break and are stitched back together wrongly, often all at once
Step 3 · The damage

Bone-forming cells grow out of control

  • Near the growth plate: over half of tumors start around the knee
  • Bad bone: the tumor lays down weak, immature bone
  • New blood supply: it grows its own vessels (VEGFR2), the target of cabozantinib and regorafenib
  • Hides from immunity: few immune cells get in, which is why checkpoint drugs mostly fail
  • Resists chemo: a poor response to first chemo predicts relapse
Step 4 · The result

It spreads to the lungs

  • About 1 in 5 already has visible spread at diagnosis
  • 85–90% of that spread is in the lungs
  • 5-year survival is about 70% if it hasn't spread, 20–30% if it has
Newest clue (Aug 2026): a single-cell map of osteosarcoma found a cancer-cell state marked by ADAMTS12 that is concentrated in tumors that have spread. That makes it a possible early warning sign.
How it reaches the lungs (May 2026): researchers in Cell Discovery showed the protein ASB7 drives lung spread by destroying another protein, ATF2. Blocking it stopped spread in mice.
Genome map: one of the largest osteosarcoma sequencing datasets confirmed TP53 as the most commonly broken gene. It also showed how varied each tumor's damage is, which is why one drug rarely fits all.
