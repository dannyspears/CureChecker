Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for frontotemporal dementia (FTD) and no way to slow or prevent it, according to the U.S. National Institute on Aging. No drug is approved to slow it. FTD damages the front and side parts of the brain, changing behavior, personality, language or movement. About 60% of people with FTD are 45 to 64 years old. Most current research targets the inherited form caused by changes in the GRN gene, which lowers a protein called progranulin. The largest trial so far, latozinemab's Phase 3 INFRONT-3, raised progranulin but did not slow the disease. Our view: a treatment that slows FTD has not been shown yet. Medicines, therapy and caregiver support can ease some symptoms.

  1. No approved drug to slow FTD.Care today treats symptoms. Doctors may prescribe SSRI antidepressants for impulsive behavior, and a European expert panel recommends trazodone for restlessness.
  2. The biggest trial failed.On October 21, 2025, Alector reported that latozinemab did not slow FTD-GRN over 96 weeks, even though it raised blood progranulin by 165%. Full results came at the AAN meeting in April 2026.
  3. Two gene therapies were stopped in 2026.Lilly ended its GRN gene therapy (LY3884963, formerly PR006) in February for lack of convincing benefit. Passage Bio closed its upliFT-D trial in May after the FDA asked for a randomized trial.
  4. Two progranulin approaches are still being tested.AviadoBio began a fourth dose group of its AVB-101 gene therapy in March 2026. Denali expects DNL593 results in 2027. The FDA gave DNL593 orphan drug status in August 2026.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Care decisions belong with an FTD-experienced medical team.

A possible missing link

Please read first. This is an AI-generated hypothesis, written by Cure Checker's AI from published research. It is speculation, not an established finding. It is not a recommendation, not a prediction that a cure exists, and not medical advice. No doctors or scientists have reviewed it, and it may be wrong. Never start, stop, change or delay any treatment because of it. Talk with your FTD-experienced care team.

What looks like it is working

  • Fixing lysosomes, in mice. Progranulin works in lysosomes, the cell's recycling centers. Without it, mice lose a lysosome fat called BMP. Brain-reaching progranulin given by vein restored BMP and reduced brain damage in a study by the company developing it (Cell, 2021).
  • Better-designed gene therapy, in mice. Progranulin changed to avoid a protein called sortilin restored BMP better than the unchanged version (Sci Transl Med, 2026). Animal research only.
  • Early warning in gene carriers. In FTD gene carriers, blood neurofilament light began rising about 15 years before estimated symptom onset (Ann Neurol, 2022). In a small study, a spinal-fluid test found misfolded TDP-43 in almost half of 14 carriers without symptoms; two later developed symptoms (Alzheimers Dement, 2025).

Where the links are missing

  • Blood progranulin is not the same as brain repair. Latozinemab raised blood progranulin by 165% in FTD-GRN but did not slow the disease or change damage markers or MRI (AAN abstract, 2026). Our inference: a rise in blood progranulin does not show whether lysosomes inside brain cells were fixed.
  • No reliable lysosome test in people yet. In patients, spinal-fluid BMP was only slightly low, and more samples are needed (Cell, 2021). Registered outcomes for DNL593 and AVB-101 list progranulin and nerve-damage markers, but no lysosome fats (NCT05262023, NCT06064890). Our inference: trials may not show whether treatment reached its real target.
  • Most FTD is not GRN-FTD. Only 6.9% of 434 people with FTD-spectrum disease in one study had a GRN change (Arch Neurol, 2010). A study not yet peer-reviewed found lysosome fat changes in FTD brains without a known gene too (bioRxiv preprint, 2024). Our inference: whether this matters in sporadic FTD is untested.

Our hypothesis

Could a spinal-fluid test of lysosome health (for example, BMP levels), which does not yet exist for routine use, show whether a progranulin treatment is repairing brain cells better than blood progranulin does? And could a treatment that brings it back to normal, started in GRN carriers whose markers show the disease beginning, slow FTD where raising blood progranulin later did not?

What would prove it wrong: if spinal-fluid lysosome markers do not track the disease in GRN carriers, or if normalizing them does not slow brain shrinkage or symptoms.

Treatments that are working

No treatment slows FTD yet. Symptom medicines and therapy help some people. Here is where the disease-slowing candidates stand, including the failures.

Symptom medicines

Used today
SSRIs · trazodone · off-label

SSRI antidepressants are commonly prescribed for disinhibition and impulsive behavior. European experts recommend trazodone for restlessness. Trial evidence for these uses is limited, and none is FDA-approved for FTD.

Speech & physical therapy

Standard
Speech, physical, occupational therapists

Speech therapy with someone who knows primary progressive aphasia helps people keep communicating. Physical and occupational therapy may give modest relief from movement symptoms.

Latozinemab (AL001)

Failed Phase 3
Alector / GSK · INFRONT-3 · FTD-GRN

An antibody meant to raise progranulin. In 102 people with symptoms, it raised blood progranulin but did not slow the disease over 96 weeks. Its extension studies were stopped.

Progranulin gene therapies

Two stopped
Lilly LY3884963 · Passage Bio PBFT02

Lilly stopped LY3884963 (PR006) in February 2026 for lack of convincing benefit. Passage Bio reported encouraging biomarker results for PBFT02, then closed its trial in May 2026 after the FDA asked for a randomized trial.

AVB-101 gene therapy

In trials
AviadoBio · ASPIRE-FTD Phase 1/2 · FTD-GRN

Delivered by surgery into the thalamus. Twelve people had been dosed by March 2026, the company reported, with higher spinal-fluid progranulin at higher doses and no serious side effects linked to treatment (company release).

DNL593

In trials
Denali · Phase 1/2 · FTD-GRN

Progranulin built to cross into the brain from an IV infusion. Enrollment is complete with 40 people with FTD-GRN, and results are now expected in 2027.

Natural remedies & daily care

No supplement or lifestyle change has been shown to stop FTD. Daily routines and activity may help people live better. We rate each by the strength of evidence.

Strong expert consensus Limited / mixed evidence No evidence, or possible harm

Helpful daily care

Calm, structured routines

The NIA advises a regular schedule, fewer distractions, simple choices, and not arguing with behavior the person cannot control.

Physical & mental activity

In 105 people carrying FTD genes, those who were more active declined more slowly. This was an observational study, so it cannot prove activity caused the difference.

PEA with luteolin

One small Italian Phase 2 trial (48 people) found less decline than placebo over 24 weeks. Not yet confirmed. Check with the care team before using it.

Promoted without evidence, or risky

Alzheimer's drug memantine

A randomized trial in 81 people found no benefit in FTD and more memory and thinking side effects than placebo.

"Brain-boosting" supplements

No supplement has been shown to slow or prevent FTD. The NIA advises checking with a doctor before adding or stopping any supplement.

Unsupervised eating

Compulsive eating is common in behavioral-variant FTD. The NIA suggests supervising meals, limiting food choices and locking cabinets if needed.

Be careful: before diagnosis, people with FTD usually cannot see that their behavior has changed, and they cannot control it. To keep everyone safe, caregivers may have to take on new responsibilities or arrange extra care. Ask the care team for help planning.

What causes frontotemporal dementia

In FTD, nerve cells in the frontal and temporal lobes stop working and die. The proteins tau and TDP-43 build up in abnormal forms. In most cases the cause is unknown, but about one in three cases is inherited.

Step 1 · The trigger

Gene change or unknown cause

~1 in 3 inheritedmost: cause unknown
  • MAPT gene: changes the tau protein
  • GRN gene: lowers progranulin, so TDP-43 goes wrong
  • C9orf72 gene: the most common gene change in family FTD and family ALS
Step 2 · The tipping point

Proteins go wrong

  • Tau and TDP-43 normally help cells work
  • In FTD they take abnormal forms, for reasons not fully understood
Step 3 · The damage

Frontal and temporal lobes

  • Frontal lobe: damage there is linked to behavior changes
  • Temporal lobe: damage there affects language and emotions
  • Memory areas: often spared at first
Step 4 · The result

Symptoms that worsen

  • Changes in behavior and personality, language, or movement
  • Symptoms get worse over time. Some people live more than 10 years after diagnosis, others less than two
Raising progranulin was not enough (Oct 2025): latozinemab raised blood progranulin by 165% but did not slow FTD-GRN or brain shrinkage. Researchers are now asking whether progranulin has to be raised inside the brain instead.
How long people live (Aug 2026): in 278 people with genetic FTD in the European–Canadian GENFI study, median survival was about 6.9 years from first symptoms. Movement problems and older age at onset were linked to shorter survival.
Better gene therapy design (Aug 2026): in mice, a progranulin gene therapy changed so it avoids the protein sortilin worked better than the unchanged version. This is animal research only.
