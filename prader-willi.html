Cure Checker's conclusion · Oct 2026

Most recent cure information

There is no cure for Prader-Willi syndrome (PWS). It is caused by missing genes on chromosome 15 that affect the hypothalamus, the brain's appetite and hormone center, so people feel constant, extreme hunger. On March 26, 2025 the FDA approved Vykat XR (diazoxide choline) for hyperphagia, the first drug for it, in people 4 and older. Growth hormone, strict food-environment control and therapy remain the base of care. Our view: the cure will need to restore the silent genes, which is still lab-stage. Until then, controlling hunger and protecting health is where the progress is.

  1. First approved hunger drug.Vykat XR won FDA approval on March 26, 2025 for hyperphagia in people 4 and older with PWS.
  2. The picture is more mixed than the headline.The 127-person Phase 3 trial missed its main goal overall but showed benefit in people with severe hunger. A 16-week withdrawal period found that those switched to placebo got hungrier.
  3. Benefits held for two years.At ENDO 2026, long-term data in 77 people showed hunger scores stayed improved at two years, including for those who restarted after a placebo period.
  4. Not every approach works.In July 2026, the Phase 3 COMPASS PWS trial of carbetocin nasal spray found no difference from placebo at 12 weeks.

This is Cure Checker's editorial assessment of published evidence, not medical advice. Care decisions belong with a PWS-experienced medical team.

Treatments that are working

One drug is approved for hunger. Growth hormone, structured food environments, therapies and hormone care make up the rest of care.

Vykat XR (diazoxide choline)

FDA approved
Approved Mar 26, 2025 · ages 4 and older · hyperphagia

An extended-release tablet. The Phase 3 trial (127 people) missed its main goal overall but helped those with severe hunger. Common side effects are excess hair growth, swelling and high blood sugar, so blood sugar must be monitored.

Long-term results

2-year data
C614 extension · 77 people · ENDO 2026

Improvements in hunger and behavior held for two years, and BMI stayed stable in those who restarted treatment after the placebo period.

Growth hormone

Standard
Somatropin · started early in childhood

Improves growth, muscle and body composition, and is part of standard care in many countries.

Carbetocin nasal spray

Mixed
CARE-PWS and COMPASS PWS Phase 3

An oxytocin-like spray. CARE-PWS reported benefit in ages 7–18. The later COMPASS PWS trial found no difference from placebo at 12 weeks.

Structured environment

Standard
Food security · routines

Locked or controlled food access, scheduled meals and consistent routines are the foundation of weight and behavior care.

Early therapy

Standard
Physical, speech, occupational

Starting in infancy builds muscle strength, feeding and communication skills.

Natural remedies & daily care

No supplement stops PWS hunger. The most effective daily tools are about the environment. We rate each by the strength of evidence.

Strong expert consensus Limited / mixed evidence No evidence, or possible harm

Helpful daily care

Controlled food access

Locks, supervision and consistent meal times are the most reliable way to prevent obesity.

Daily physical activity

Regular movement supports muscle and weight.

Predictable routines

Structure reduces anxiety and behavior outbursts for many people.

Promoted without evidence, or risky

Appetite-suppressant supplements

Unproven in PWS and may be unsafe. Check with the care team first.

Fad or very-low-calorie diets

People with PWS need a dietitian-led plan, because needs differ from the general population.

Relying on willpower

The hunger is neurological. Environmental control works better than appeals to self-control.

Be careful: people with PWS can have a high pain tolerance and may not show fever or vomiting normally. Treat unexplained illness seriously and see a doctor.

What causes Prader-Willi syndrome

PWS happens when a region of chromosome 15 inherited from the father (15q11-q13) is missing or switched off. Several genes there are normally active only on the father's copy, so nothing replaces them.

Step 1 · The gene region

Paternal genes are missing

~1 in 15,000–25,000births
  • Usually not inherited: most cases happen by chance in the egg or sperm
  • Two main causes: a missing piece of the father's chromosome 15, or two copies from the mother
  • Mirror condition: the same region on the mother's copy causes Angelman syndrome
Step 2 · The tipping point

The hypothalamus is affected

  • The hypothalamus controls appetite, growth, temperature and sleep
  • Fewer oxytocin-producing neurons have been found in people with PWS
Step 3 · The damage

Hunger that never stops

  • Infancy: weak muscle tone and poor feeding
  • Childhood onward: extreme hunger (hyperphagia)
  • Hormones: low growth hormone and sex hormones
  • Learning & behavior: developmental delay, anxiety, rigidity
Step 4 · The result

Health risks

  • Without food-access control, severe obesity, diabetes and heart problems can follow
  • Food security, supervision and medicine reduce the risk
A brain-based hunger, not willpower: the hunger in PWS comes from how the brain is wired. It is not a choice or a parenting failure.
Why gene therapy is hard: the right genes are present but silenced on the mother's copy, so one idea is to switch them back on. This is still in the lab.
New drug targets (2026): a 2026 review of obesity treatment in PWS surveys the pipeline beyond appetite drugs.
